# M11 P1: Karma Render Settings, core

Package P1 of [M11.md](M11.md): every parameter of the Karma Render Settings LOP (`karmarendersettings`) that has
a Nova equivalent takes effect in Nova when Nova's own setting is not authored, and a scene set up with that LOP
renders like Karma where Nova's defaults differed. Research: [karma-properties.md](../research/m11/karma-properties.md)
(WP-A, WP-C, section 3) and [karma-lops.md](../research/m11/karma-lops.md) (K1). All measurements below are Karma CPU
22.0.466 through isolated husk (`tests/reference/tools/refcommon.IsolatedHoudini`), the scenes of
`tests/husk/karma_props/`.

## 1. The fallback rule

**Authored, not "different from the default".** Hydra fills every setting a delegate describes with the
descriptor's default, so `GetRenderSetting` cannot tell an authored `nova:samples = 64` from no value at all; the
old fallbacks ("Karma's value while Nova's equals its default") let Karma win over an explicit Nova value equal to
the default. The delegate now records which keys the host set: the keys of the settings map handed to the
constructor and every `SetRenderSetting` with a non-empty value (`HdNovaRenderDelegate::IsSettingAuthored`,
`GetAuthoredSetting`; `TF_DEBUG=HDNOVA_SETTINGS` prints what arrives). Hydra's own default fill-in goes through
`_PopulateDefaultSettings`, which is not recorded.

**Order.** Render-wide: Nova's setting if authored, else Karma's if authored, else the default: Nova's, or, for the
properties where Karma's defaults differ from Nova's, Karma's default when the render settings are Karma's
(`HasKarmaSettings()`: some `karma:global:*` is authored, which the Karma Render Settings LOP always does: it
authors 87 Karma values at their defaults). Per object: the prim's Nova primvar, the prim's Karma primvar (Render
Geometry Settings LOP), the render settings' Karma value (`HdNovaObjectDefaults`), the default.

So: a scene with only Nova's LOPs renders as before (the Nova LOPs author every Nova setting); a scene with only
the Karma Render Settings LOP renders with Karma's values and Karma's defaults; with both, Nova's settings win
setting by setting and Karma's fill the rest.

**Value forms.** The Karma Render Settings LOP authors menus as their labels (`"Rounded Curves"`, `"Variance"`,
`"Disable Displacement Shader"`), Render Geometry Settings as ints, switches as bools; the pixel oracle is JSON.
`hydra/karmaSettings.h` reads all of them (`HdNovaMenuIndex`, `HdNovaFlag`, `HdNovaNumber`,
`HdNovaParseKarmaPixelOracle`). This fixed the menu-label bug of `curve_style`/`point_style` on the render
settings (research finding 5).

**Object defaults reach the prims** through `HdNovaRenderParam::SetObjectDefaults` (set by the render pass before
the first sync and compared every Execute; a change after prims used them re-syncs meshes, curves, points and
volumes, as the velocity defaults of M8 D19 do).

## 3. Karma's behaviour, measured

### 3.0 What the current LOP authors

The research dumped the hidden v1 node. `karmarendersettings` (re-measured, `scratch dump_krs.py`) authors 87
attributes at defaults, among them colour limits **20**/20 (v1: 10), `causticsenable` 0 and
`causticsroughnessclamp` 0.2 explicitly, `constrainmaxrough` 1, `ssslimit` **1** (v1: 0), `karma:global:enable_dof`,
`karma:global:image_blur`, `karma:object:mblur` 1, menus as labels; no `pixelfiltersize` (only v1 does), no
`randomseed`, `mtlx_image*`, `internalreflectenable`, `treat_as_lightsource`. Its toggles author:
Enable Depth of Field `karma:global:enable_dof`, Enable Motion Blur `disableMotionBlur`, Enable Image Blur
`karma:global:image_blur`, Force Headlight `karma:global:disable_lighting`.

### 3.1 Sampling

Karma's render stats (husk's `husk:render_stats` in the EXR) on `sampling_*` (192 x 115):

| Case | camera rays / px | indirect rays at the first hit / px |
|---|---|---|
| defaults (Variance, 9 primary, max 9 secondary, oracle variance 0.01) | 4.87 | 37.0 (7.6 per camera ray) |
| 4 primary, max 6 secondary, oracle 0.02 | 2.52 | 15.1 (6.0 per camera ray) |
| Path Traced 24, uniform | 24.0 | 24.0 |

Nova traces one path per sample, so a Karma camera ray with n secondary rays is n Nova samples: Variance and
Distributed map to primary x max secondary samples with Nova's variance oracle deciding where to stop (Karma's
oracle threshold, or with a uniform oracle `varianceaa_thresh` and at least primary x min secondary samples); Path
Traced maps one to one. Results: section 6.

### 3.2 Fake caustics

A glass plane (SS glass, IOR 1.5, smooth) over a floor under a distant light at angle theta from the vertical,
ratio of the floor under the plane to the floor without it (`scratch fake_probe.py`; mu = cos theta, angles 0, 30,
45, 60, 70, 80):

| Crossing | 0 | 30 | 45 | 60 | 70 | 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| back face (the ray leaves the plane's front side) | 0.9600 | 0.9600 | 0.9579 | 0.9300 | 0.8416 | 0.5901 |
| front face | 0.9216 | 0.9215 | 0.9176 | 0.8649 | 0.7083 | 0.3482 |
| Schlick 1 - F | 0.9600 | 0.9600 | 0.9579 | 0.9300 | 0.8416 | 0.5901 |

So per crossing 1 - F with Schlick's F (exact to four digits), and on the side where the surface reflects another
1 - F. The glass scene shows the inside's side: with `internalreflectenable` on, the sphere's fake shadow drops by
0.96 (`glass_internal`/`glass_base` 0.960), so the second factor belongs to the reflection lobe, which Karma has
inside only with internal reflection. The controls, per crossing t:

| Control | Measured (back face) | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `fakecausticscolor` 0.5 | 0.4800 ... 0.2950 | t x colour |
| `fakecausticsopacity` 0.5 | 0.9800 ... 0.7950 | 1 - o + t o |
| `fakecausticsbsdfenable` 0, colour 0.5 / 1 | 0.5000 / 1.0000 at every angle | colour alone |
| `fakecausticsfresneldarken` d = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1 | at mu = 0.5: x0.75, x0.50, x0.25, 0 | x mu / ((1/(1-d) - 2)(1 - mu) + 1) (Schlick's bias with 1 - d; exact at all angles) |

Nova (`core/bsdf/closure.h` `fakeCausticFactor`, `karmaTransmittance`, `fakeCausticDarken`): the style
(`FakeCausticsStyle`) is Karma's when the switch came from Karma's `causticsenable` (prim or settings) or from a
Karma scene's default, Nova's physical transmittance when it came from `nova:object:fakeCaustics`; the controls
apply to both. Surfaces without transmission stay opaque whatever the controls (an assumption: the probes used
glass).

### 3.3 Internal reflection

Off (Karma's default): seen from the inside, a dielectric has no Fresnel reflection and transmits without the
Fresnel loss (research A3: a Karma slab transmits 0.96, not 0.96^2); total internal reflection stays (with it
ending the path, Nova's rough glass lost up to 30 % at its rim against Karma's `mtl_glass` reference; with it kept
the rim is within 3-7 % in 8 x 8 blocks and the reference passes). `DielectricLobe::noInternal`, set by
`dropInternalReflection` (closure.h) on the inside's lobes (the surface models face the frames to wo and give the
inside's lobes the inverse index), which also drops reflection-only dielectric lobes of the inside.

### 3.4 Colour limits

`geo_clamp.usda`: a camera-visible emitter (radiance 20), the floor it lights (emission found by BSDF rays), the
floor under a small sphere light (light samples), a region lit by bounced light only. Region means:

| Case | emitter | near the emitter | under the light | farther | far wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| limits off (1e6) | 20.000 | 0.4603 | 3.834 | 0.4625 | 0.0813 |
| the LOP's 20 / 20 | 20.000 | 0.4603 | 3.834 | 0.4625 | 0.0813 |
| 1 (linked) | **1.000** | **0.1076** | **1.056** | 0.3727 | 0.0792 |
| indirect 0.5, unlinked | 20.000 | **0.0878** | 3.465 | 0.3670 | 0.0791 |
| colorlimit 0.5, unlinked | **0.500** | 0.4603 | **0.899** | 0.4625 | 0.0813 |

So `colorlimit` clamps what the camera sees directly and the light samples of the first hit (the research's "direct
light is not clamped" came from a dim scene whose samples stayed under the limit); `colorlimitindirect`
(`colorlimit` when linked) clamps everything after the first bounce, including emission of objects found by the
first hit's BSDF ray. Nova: `RenderSettings::clampDirect` and `clampEmissionIndirect`; the integrator's clamps per
depth are `clampLight` (lights and treated emitters reached by rays), `clampEmission` (other emission) and
`clampNee` (CPU `ShadeContext`, GPU `clamp*At`). Nova's own model is unchanged (no direct clamp, depth-1 emission
direct): with the defaults the clamps are bit-identical to before.

### 3.5 Constrained roughness

`geo_rough.usda`: a rough metal floor (0.35) reflects a mirror sphere that reflects a rect light. Without the
constraint Karma's streak (camera - floor - sphere - light) is narrower: its core x1.05, its sides x0.93. Nova keeps
the roughest glossy alpha of the path (`WaveQueues::pathAlpha`, GPU `GpuWave::pathAlpha`) and raises the glossy
lobes of later hits to it (`clampLobeRoughness`); diffuse events do not count (after a diffuse event Karma's default
fake caustics skip glossy lobes anyway). The same function applies `causticsroughnessclamp` (as GGX alpha r^2) at
hits after a diffuse event on objects with true caustics.

### 3.6 Per-object motion blur

`karma:object:mblur` 0 on the moving sphere of `geo_basic.usda` renders it exactly as with motion blur disabled
(region mean 0.01394 both, blurred 0.01687). Nova: `HdNovaObjectShutter` gives such prims the frame's
instantaneous shutter (meshes, curves, points, spheres, volumes).

## 4. Warnings

`_WarnUnsupportedKarmaSettings` (once per delegate, i.e. per stage render, per property) when an authored Karma
value differs from the one Nova's image matches: outlines, path guiding, the lighting overrides
(`disable_lighting`, `override_lighting`), `image_blur` off, sample filters, the global AOV material, the dicing
camera, `overrideobject`, `raybias`, custom component labels, linear blur, `samplesfromstage` off, backface
culling, forced curve bases, displacement as bump, `dicingpredisplace`, `dicingzimportance`, `limitcolor`,
nested dielectrics, portals, `bumpshadowterminator`, `smoothp` off, `cuspangle`, stencil maps, image filters
other than the denoiser (tonemap, OCIO, background), dicing depth ranges.
Not warned: performance and noise-only settings (buckets, caches, engine, Russian roulette, screen door, light
sampling quality, the `*quality` multipliers); `volumesteprate` (Nova's unbiased tracking is documented, M8 D16:
finer rates converge to Nova's image); holdouts and ray visibility (P2).

The Karma Render Settings LOP's defaults (Variance, 9 x 9, oracle 0.01) give Nova 20.6 samples per pixel on the
basic scene where Karma traces 37 paths (4.9 camera rays x 7.6 secondary): Nova's oracle stops earlier, which is
within its noise model (M7 D4); 4 x 6 at 0.02 agrees (16.1 vs 15.1).

Golden scenes: the reference scenes are Karma scenes in this sense, since `tests/reference/scenes/common.usda`
authors Karma settings (Path Traced, colour limits off, true caustics, no constrained roughness: Karma set up to
match Nova). Its one Karma default that differs from Nova's and that it does not author, `internalreflectenable`
(off), now applies, as it did in their Karma references: `mtl_glass`, `limits_caustics`, `furnace_materials` and
`aov_components` change, the other 30 are bit-identical, and with `nova:object:internalReflection` authored on
the four are bit-identical to before too (checked), so nothing else of the Karma settings changes them.
`[gpu]`: the new XPU case agrees with the CPU (the `[gpu]` set passes). Viewport (`run_viewport_check.ps1 -Only karmasettings`): renders, and shows that the
viewport hands Nova all of its display options (section 7).

## 7. Deviations and open items

- **The viewport** sends every display option of Nova's `_Viewport.ds` with each update (measured with
  `TF_DEBUG=HDNOVA_SETTINGS`: `nova:samples`, `pixelFilter`, `pixelFilterScale`, `clampIndirect`, `clampDirect`,
  `maxDepth`, the diffuse/reflect/refract limits, `dicingQualityScale`, `textureFilterWidth`, `iprProxyDepth`,
  `viewportDenoise`, `denoiseStartPasses`, `subdivLevel`), so in the viewport these count as authored and the
  viewport's Nova options win over a Karma render settings prim's values for them (as Karma's own display options
  are Karma's viewport settings). The look-relevant one is the clamp (the viewport keeps Nova's 10 instead of
  Karma's colour limits); fake caustics, internal reflection, constrained roughness, per-object properties and
  everything else of the Karma settings apply in the viewport too. husk and batch renders use Karma's values.
- True caustics through the glass ball: under the sphere's rim Nova's floor is darker than Karma's (ring 0.0065 vs
  0.0227, the ball's rim 0.021 vs 0.031) while the focus agrees within noise; P1 does not change Nova's true-caustic
  paths (only their roughness clamp), so this is a difference of the caustic paths themselves, to be measured
  separately. Nova's free (unconstrained) glossy
  streak is 8 % narrower than Karma's at its sides, and Nova's flat ribbons shade 6 % brighter (M9); the checks
  allow for these with wider tolerances.
- Under a bright light's spot Karma's indirect clamp at 1 cuts the bounce light a little more than Nova's (region
  1.056 vs 1.105); elsewhere the clamps agree within 2 %.
- Existing tests whose scenes author Karma settings now get Karma's values for what Nova does not author:
  `houdini_volume_karma` renders with Karma's box filter (its settings author `pixelfilter box`), which moves the
  fire-lit smoke at volume limit 0 from 4.6 % to 6.2 % under Karma's (M8's known single-scattering difference;
  the tolerance went from 6 to 7 %, the shading defaults change nothing there, checked).
- Karma's colour limits were measured in Path Traced mode; in Variance mode Karma may clamp the average of a camera
  ray's secondary rays rather than each.
- The fake-caustic controls on surfaces without transmission, and Fresnel darken with the BSDF switch off, were
  not measured (Nova: opaque; darken applies).
- Split reflection/transmission lobes (`transmission_extra_roughness`) take Karma's factor on top of their own
  Fresnel share.
- `image_blur`, the camera's exposure/tint/window (WP-B) and P9's features only warn.
