# M9 design: curves and hair, subsurface scattering, many lights

Status: binding design for M9. docs/PLAN.md asks for "Haare/Kurven (Embree-Curves, Chiang-Hair-BSDF), SSS Random Walk, Light-BVH, Light-Linking", with the result "Feature-Parität mit dem Arnold-Kern". Items deferred to M9 by earlier milestones: light sampling of emissive meshes (M3), equiangular sampling for point and spot lights in thin media (M8 D-deferred), per-light sampling quality (M7).

Research, measured on this machine (Houdini 22.0.466, isolated hython/husk only):
- `docs/research/m9/sss.md`: random-walk SSS, Karma's parametrization and boundary model (47 Karma calibration scenes, a lab of six experiments, `probes/sss_*`);
- `docs/research/m9/curves-hair.md`: what Houdini authors and Hydra hands over for curves, Karma's curve conventions, Karma Hair = pbrt's Chiang model (89 Karma scenes, a Hydra probe delegate, a Chiang prototype, `probes/ch_*`);
- `docs/research/m9/lights.md`: light tree vs Nova's alias table, BSDF-ray light queries, lights at infinity, Karma's emissive meshes as lights and light linking, what Hydra hands a third-party delegate (a light lab, a probe delegate, Karma scenes, `probes/lt_*`).

## 1. Decisions

The user asked to continue until the renderer is done; as in M4-M8 the research's recommended options are taken. The user may revise any of them. The Solaris UI follows Karma's nodes (M6.7). Karma CPU is the measured reference: Arnold is not available here, and Karma Hair, Karma's SSS and its light tree are the Arnold-like features PLAN.md means (see 2.3).

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Curve data path | **Curves are a mesh shape** (`MeshShape::Curves`) on Embree's curve types, reusing the record machinery (materials, attributes, instances, motion, LPE tags, limits, cryptomatte). `HdBasisCurves` is expanded on the host: pinned B-spline/Catmull-Rom get the USD phantoms `2 P0 - P1`, `2 Pn-1 - Pn-2` (Karma, measured; not Hdsi's end repetition); periodic curves repeat 1 (linear, Bezier) or 3 points; varying primvars and widths map to segment ends. `nurbsCurves` render as their linear control hull (Karma). `centripetalCatmullRom` renders as uniform Catmull-Rom with one warning (Houdini never authors it). `points` render as `SPHERE_POINT` / `DISC_POINT` / `ORIENTED_DISC_POINT` by `karma:object:point_style` (default spheres). |
| D2 | Width and style | **Width is the diameter** (Karma, exact to 1/10 px), times `karma:object:width_scale` (render setting, constant primvar); no minimum width. Without widths: width 1 (USD's fallback). **`karma:object:curve_style`** (render setting, constant primvar; `nova:object:curveStyle` alias) with Karma's default **Rounded: authored normals are ignored**. 0: camera-facing ribbons (`FLAT_*`, N = −ray), 1: round tubes (`ROUND_*`, linear with neighbour flags: round caps at curve ends, open cubic ends), 2 and 3: normal-oriented ribbons for cubic curves; linear curves cannot be oriented and fall back to flat (Embree, as Karma). |
| D3 | The curve hit | N: round = the tube normal; flat = −ray; oriented = the interpolated authored normal (Ng Embree's). Tangent root to tip in `SurfaceHit`. `uv` = (0, v) with v uniform per segment over the whole curve (Karma's `ray:hituv`); `prim` for AOVs = the curve index (`ray:primid` = `ray:element`). Attributes: constant; uniform per curve; vertex interpolated with the basis; varying linear between segment ends. Default material: Nova's (M3 K19) on the tube normal. Hair offset h = N · (z × T), z = wo projected into the normal plane (reproduces Karma's per-pixel profile). |
| D4 | Ray offsets on round curves | Rays leaving a round-curve hit start at the fibre's **exit point**: reflection side P + (1e-3 r + ULPs) N; transmission side the cylinder chord exit along the direction plus the same epsilon; shadow rays alike. Flat curves keep Embree's 2r avoidance, oriented ribbons offset along ±Ng like triangles. |
| D5 | Hair BSDF | **Chiang 2016 in pbrt's formulation as four `LobeType::Hair` lobes** (R, TT, TRT, TRRT+) in the ordinary `ClosureList`, everything wo-dependent computed at the hit: per lobe {p, v_p, s, tilted sin/cos θ, Φ_p + φ_o}; weight tint_p · A_p; estimate luminance(weight); frame (T, z × T, z). Karma's conventions (measured): β_m, β_n → v_R = (0.726 β + 0.812 β² + 3.7 β²⁰)², v_TT = v/4, v_TRT = v_TRRT = 4v; s = √(π/8)(0.265 β_n + 1.194 β_n² + 5.372 β_n²²); **pbrt's 10-term I0 series** and log form; d'Eon's M_p sampler (sampled weights are exactly Σ A_p: furnace 1); tilt α = −shift · 90° applied to θ_i (R −2α, TT +α, TRT +4α); TRRT+ azimuthally uniform. **Events are glossy and classified by the side of the tube normal**: reflect if wi is on wo's side, else refract (LPE `<RG>`/`<TG>` and the reflect/refract limits; never diffuse); the light's specular multiplier; never enters a medium; opaque to shadow rays and fake caustics. |
| D6 | Hair materials | **`kma_hair`** (Karma Hair) natively with the measured formulas: σ_a = absorption + Chiang's colour inversion of base·baseColor at β_n + thicknessScale · (−ln(1 − melanin)) · [(1 − redness) · 1.2085 · (0.419, 0.697, 1.37) + redness · 1.833 · (0.187, 0.4, 1.05)], times nothing (tint multiplies the result as an overall weight); β_m = roughness, β_n = roughnessAzimuthal if anisotropic else roughness; TT/TRT scales ttscale/trtscale; ior; `diffuse` mixes a Lambert(diffuseColor) on the tube normal (`<RD>`); melanin/roughness randomize from a hash of the curve index; `coat` warns and is ignored (its model is open). **`kma_fur`**: the same with medulla radius 0 (help: equivalent to Karma Hair); medulla, wetness and the second R lobe warn. **MaterialX `ND_chiang_hair_bsdf`**: a graph leaf per the MaterialX spec (per-lobe (v, s) as given with s·√(π/8), tints, cuticle_angle → α = (cuticle − 0.5) π with Karma's sign, `curve_direction` → T) with the same lobes; Karma's own lowering of this node is not Chiang (furnace 0.04-0.06) and is not followed (documented difference). |
| D7 | SSS model | **Karma's random walk** (measured, lab within 0.1-0.4 %): cosine entry about −N_g, index-matched walk with HG(g), σ_t = 1/mfp per channel with mfp = max(radius · scale, 1e-5) (OpenPBR radius_scale · radius), the single-scattering albedo from the van de Hulst/OpenPBR inverse with the anisotropy correction (`core/bsdf/subsurface.h`), Lambertian exit on the exit's **shading normal** (option B of research 5.3), no Fresnel at the exit (the specular layer scales the SSS once at the entry, as the closures already do), a cap of **256 segments** (killed: Karma's energy loss at A ≥ 0.95 and g = 0.9), Russian roulette below a max-channel throughput of 0.1. Chromatic mean free paths: one walk, channel per segment by throughput, balance-heuristic spectral MIS (exact single channel when achromatic). Misses mean no boundary (open meshes are half spaces). Thin-walled: unchanged translucency. Walk queries see only the hit's own mesh instance (`IAccel::intersectLocal` on its BLAS in object space; hit filters ignored). |
| D8 | SSS in the closures | `LobeType::Subsurface` {σ_t[3], α[3], g}: no value at its entry (excluded from NEE, the mixture density and light-sample LPE parts, like a delta lobe); picked as a continuation it yields a walk sample of class SSS with weight w/p. Standard Surface and OpenPBR (thick) and the generic graph leaf (`subsurface_bsdf` now carries radius and anisotropy: 8 floats) push it. Pick estimate and albedo AOV: weight × colour, as before. |
| D9 | SSS in the integrator | A new pass **C'' `walkSubsurface`** after pass C: the queued walks (entry point, oriented Ng, mesh, instance, medium, ray) run to completion, one thread per walk (the shape of one CUDA kernel in M10). At the exit: one light sample with the exit's Lambert lobe (light diffuse multiplier, MIS with cos/π) and a cosine continuation of class SSS (`bsdfPdf` cos/π, `diffuseShare` 1, cone as after a diffuse event), RR after the walk. A path may now have two light samples per bounce (entry and exit): the shadow queue holds two per path. Streams (determinism as M8 D18): entry direction and first flight from `bounce(depth, kWalk)`, the rest from a PCG stream seeded with that id, the exit's light sample from the new block `stream::walkLight(depth)` (0x60000), the continuation from `bounce(depth, kBsdf).xy` (unused at the entry), RR from `bounce(depth, kRoulette)`. Walk segments count as rays; walks killed at the cap are counted (`subsurfaceWalkStats`, `TF_DEBUG` `HDNOVA_SSS`). |
| D10 | SSS limits and LPE | Unchanged semantics (Karma measured): a walk is **one SSS event** at its entry (`<TD.'sss'>`); past the SSS limit the walk still runs and its exit gathers lights only (light-only continuation); the diffuse limit does not apply. The exit adds no LPE event. `sss_traceset` (walks across objects sharing a name) is deferred: it warns once. |
| D11 | Light selection | **A pbrt-v4-style light tree** (BVH over light bounds and direction cones) over every bounded light: sphere, rect, disk, cylinder, point, spot, volume lights, emissive triangles (D14), instanced lights (D16). One light per leaf, one sample per shading point (no splitting: 0.23-0.83x efficiency, research E7). Importance with a **one-sided** receiver bound for closures without transmission, two-sided with transmission, none for volume events; d² clamp with the squared half diagonal. Selection reuses `kLight`.z rescaled per level. The MIS PMF of a light hit by a BSDF ray comes from its **bit trail** evaluated at the **previous vertex** (new path state: previous normal, oct-encoded, and a transmits bit; the position is `rays.org`). Build: SAOH, 12 buckets, parallel; value edits refit, moves/added/removed lights rebuild. `karma:global:light_sampling_mode` = Uniform keeps today's alias table (debug). |
| D12 | Lights at infinity | Outside the tree, chosen with **q = I_inf / (I_inf + Σ_cut I_node)** over a 16-node cut at the receiver (1.04-1.43x of the oracle everywhere, E5); among themselves by Nova's power alias table. The BSDF-hit PMF of an infinite light uses the same q at the previous vertex. |
| D13 | BSDF rays finding lights | `areaLightsAlongRay`, `areaLightCrossings` (volumes) and `nearestCameraLight` **traverse the light tree's boxes** (all crossings, stack) instead of looping over every light (0.1-1.9 µs vs 2.3 µs-1 ms per ray, E4). Lights still never block rays. Emissive triangles need no query (they are ordinary surface hits). |
| D14 | Emissive meshes as lights | `karma:object:treat_as_lightsource` (0/1/2 Auto = when the material emits; Karma's registry default 0) with `lightsource_diffusescale/specularscale/samplingquality` as constant primvars, and `nova:object:lightSource` alias (as volumes, M8 D13). Emitters: each triangle whose material slot emits (uniform non-zero emission, or a varying emission sub-program `Program::emission` that is not identically zero), per instance, world space, **two-sided** (Karma measured), power φ = 2π · lum(E) · A (varying: the sub-program at centroid and corners at commit). NEE: area sampling, spherical-triangle sampling above a solid-angle threshold; the shadow ray stores (mesh, instance, prim, barycentrics) and **emission is evaluated only at unoccluded shadow-ray ends**, in a pass after `traceShadowRays`, batched per material. BSDF hits on a treated emitter are MIS-weighted with the tree PMF at the previous vertex. Treated emitters are **L events everywhere** (camera too: `CL`), the object's lpetag is the light tag; untreated ones stay O with weight 1. The two scales act as the light's diffuse/specular multipliers on receivers, never on the camera's view. A budget warning above 2 M emitter triangles. |
| D15 | Light linking | **pxr's `HdsiLightLinkingSceneIndex`** appended in Nova's scene-index plugin (geometry types mesh, sphere, volume, basisCurves, points; **not `instancer`**: per-instance categories would be lost, probe). `HdNovaLight` reads `lightLink`/`shadowLink` categories, rprims `GetCategories`/instance categories. Core: category ids → **receiver sets** per object instance (distinct category combinations, 0 = none, any number of them, each with its 64-bit category mask); per light the bit of the one category it links to (all bits: it lights every receiver), per tree node the OR of its linked lights' bits and a flag for an unlinked light below; a light lights a set iff it lights everything or the set holds its category, and a child none of whose lights lights the receiver's set has importance 0 (exact PMF for any number of sets, nothing wasted on rejections). Checks (Karma measured): NEE selects within the receiver's set; BSDF/phase rays reaching a light (area, infinite, treated emitter, volume-light emission) count only if the light lights the **previous vertex's** set (4 bytes of path state); linking acts on direct light only (indirect light via linked objects arrives). Shadow linking: an occluder blocks light i only if it is in i's shadow set (an occlusion filter on excluded objects; excluded volumes skip their transmittance); **lights with a non-trivial `shadowLink` are NEE-only** (BSDF weight 0, light weight 1; their light samples cover their whole emission, so unbiased), **except domes**, whose light samples cover only their distribution's support: a shadow-linked dome's BSDF strategy is a **shadow ray of its own** along the continuation of every non-delta event (its shadow set as the occluders, like its light samples), MIS-weighted against them, and continuations of non-delta events never collect it (continuations of delta events, which no light sample can produce, keep collecting shadow-linked lights with weight 1, every object occluding them; documented). The camera is not a receiver. |
| D16 | Instanced lights | A light sprim with an instancer id becomes one `LightDesc` per instance (world = instance × light), re-synced on `DirtyInstancer` (Karma lights every instance, measured). |
| D17 | Equiangular sampling | Opt-in per light (`inputs:karma:light:equiangular`, default off; `nova:light:equiangular`): point, spot, sphere and small area lights get equiangular distance sampling along the continuation in volumes, combined with free flights by MIS whose weights come from a deterministic proxy of the free-flight density (unbiased with spectral tracking). Its own PCG stream (M8 D18 scheme). Tested on M8 E6's homogeneous single-scattering cases against the closed form. |
| D18 | Karma light properties | `inputs:karma:light:samplingquality` (default 1, the M7-deferred per-light quality) multiplies the light's selection weight in the tree or the outside table; `inputs:karma:light:samplingmode` Uniform puts it into the outside table; `karma:global:light_sampling_mode` Uniform disables the tree (debug); `karma:global:light_sampling_quality` is read and documented as a no-op for one-sample selection. Legacy `category`/`shadowtraceset`/`lightcategories` warn. |

## 2. Scope

### 2.3 Deviations from PLAN.md

- "Feature-Parität mit dem Arnold-Kern": Arnold is not installed, so parity is measured against Karma CPU, whose hair (Chiang, mirroring Arnold's `standard_hair` controls), random-walk SSS and light tree are the corresponding features (research). Arnold's `randomwalk_v2` boundary and Arnold-specific controls are not reproduced.
- "Light-BVH": a light tree in pbrt-v4's form (a BVH over lights with direction cones): the same thing, named as the literature does.
- PLAN.md's pass list has no SSS stage; D9 adds pass C'' (the M10 GPU backend gets the same stage as one kernel).
- MaterialX `ND_chiang_hair_bsdf` follows the MaterialX specification, not Karma's (non-Chiang) lowering of it (D6): a documented difference from Karma.

### 3.2 Pass structure per depth

`intersectRays` → `traceMedia` → pass A `gatherLights` (tree-PMF MIS at the previous vertex, link checks) → pass B → pass C `shadeHits` (tree NEE; SSS entries queue walks) → pass C'' `walkSubsurface` → pass C' `shadeVolumes` → `traceShadowRays` (shadow-link filter, the emission of unoccluded emitter samples (D14), volume transmittance) → AO.

## 5. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Walks are noisy at high albedo (Karma too) | parity with Karma first; Dwivedi guiding later |
| Long walks stall a CPU thread's wave | walks are bounded by the cap; the GPU kernel in M10 can split if divergence shows |
| Thin-curve self-intersection | exit-point offsets (D4) and the single-fibre "no second event" test |
| Light-tree selection cost on simple scenes | measured 0.4-0.9 µs; scenes with few lights keep a tiny tree; profile against the alias table on the benchmark scenes (±2 %) |
| Light-linking scene index changes Hydra prims for every scene | only lights with collections add categories; scenes without linking take the old path (checked by the references) |
| Emissive meshes with millions of triangles | budget warning; two-level trees later |

## 6. Notes as built

### M9.1 Curves and points

- `core/geometry/curves.{h,cpp}`, the Embree curve geometries, `resolveCurveHit` and `hydra/curves.{h,cpp}` were written before the curves research came back; the Hydra rprim then took Karma's rules (D2): `karma:object:curve_style` (constant primvar, `nova:object:curveStyle`, else the render setting, else round), `karma:object:width_scale`. The default width (1 when none is authored) is USD's fallback; Houdini always authors widths.
- `points` (`hydra/points.{h,cpp}`, `MeshShape::Points`, Embree's sphere / disc / oriented-disc points by `karma:object:point_style`): per-point radius and primvars (one value per point, a primitive each).
- The face AOV of curves is the curve (`segmentCurve`), Karma's `ray:primid`/`ray:element`. Found on the way: int AOVs written as float through the generic `driver:parameters:aov:format` (not only `husk:format`) came out as 0 in husk; fixed in `hydra/aov.cpp`.
- Round linear curves end in spheres: their hits (Embree u = 0 or 1) take the 3D radial normal, and rays into them leave by the sphere's chord (`tubeRayOrigin`).
- Deferred: `nurbsCurves` (Houdini's SOP Import never authors them, research 0.1), `ray:hituv` (no Nova AOV for it yet).
- `husk_hair` checks against Karma: widths exact to 2 % down to 1/10 pixel, width scale, point area (0.03140 vs π 0.1²), round caps (0.003927) and open cubic ends, the five styles, primid 0/1/2.

### M9.2 Hair

- `core/bsdf/hair.h`: Chiang's fibre as four `LobeType::Hair` lobes; the lobe keeps wo's azimuth, so any frame whose x is the fibre works (the native model builds one around wo, the MaterialX leaf uses the graph's frame with the curve tangent). The tilt rotates theta_i: sampling draws theta_i' from M_p and subtracts k alpha, so the solid-angle density is M_p cos(theta_i') / cos(theta_i) N_p (directions past the pole are not sampled).
- Against Karma (`husk_hair`, research tables): a white fibre 1.0000 with no second event, its R side 0.1278 / 0.1478 / 0.2828 at 0 / 30 / 60 degrees (Karma 0.1276 / 0.1479 / 0.2833); absorption, melanin defaults, melanin 0.8 and base colour within 0.2 %; per light within 1.5 % except TT from behind, 1.1-1.4 % below Karma (research 8.3: Karma's TT is about 1 % above the model); the tilt's sign (l3 0.0328 / l4 0.0344, Karma's exactly); diffuse 0.1251 / 0.0398 / 0 (0.1250 / 0.0398 / 0).
- The thin-wall pass-through at the refract limit (M4, for glass) no longer applies to hair lobes: past the limit a hair event is light-only.
- Hair ball (2000 interpenetrating fibres, research 3.6): Nova 0.979 / 0.607 at limits 64 / Karma's defaults against Karma's 0.939 / 0.522. Open: Nova itself keeps 0.98 at unlimited depth (white Lambert fibres too: 0.976), the energy of rays that start inside a crossing fibre; Karma's refract limit binds about one event earlier on hair (r64t4: Karma 0.5227, Nova t3 0.5487, t4 0.6080; r4t64 agrees within the 4 % above). Not a single-fibre difference; documented, not matched.

### M9.4 Light tree

- `core/lights/light_tree.{h,cpp}`: the research lab's tree (SAOH, 12 buckets, one light per leaf, bit trails), selection and PMF in the same arithmetic (sampling returned PMF = bit-trail PMF bit-for-bit; 1e-5 apart with `1 - p` for the second child). On by default; `karma:global:light_sampling_mode` / `nova:lightSamplingMode` "uniform" (or 0) selects by power again.
- **Deviation from research 6.1:** the d² clamp is pbrt's (half the box diagonal, unsquared), as the lab measured. The suggested squared clamp blurs proximity inside large nodes: on the street (2000 lights) it gave 6x less variance than the power table, pbrt's 104x (research E1: 107x at 1k).
- Receivers: the geometric normal facing wo (opaque closures are bounded by Ng, not by the shading normal: with N the mtl_normals reference lost delta-light energy), two-sided when a lobe transmits (external closures too), none for volume events. Two exact plane tests next to the angular bounds: a box entirely below an opaque receiver's tangent plane, and a receiver behind every emitter of a one-sided planar node, are 0 (a rect light right behind a wall otherwise took 99 % of the samples; `Camera-visible lights on a wall` test).
- Builds run in parallel (a subtree over k lights fills 2k − 1 consecutive nodes, so both children's ranges are known up front); `LightTree::update` only re-sums the powers when the lights, their boxes and cones and the masks are unchanged (intensity and colour edits), else it builds. Commits keep the emissive triangles when their meshes, materials and settings did not change. With 1 M emissive triangles (`Commit times with many emissive triangles`, `[.bench]`): first commit 4.0 → 0.85 s, a light's intensity edit 3.9 s → 0.13 s.
- Dead ends (both children 0) return no light; they are frequent for tilted receivers near the light field (up to 73 % in the mixed test street), unbiased.
- The path keeps its last scattering vertex (`prevP`, `prevN`, `prevReceiver`, `prevSet`) for the PMF of BSDF samples that reach lights; null events (fake-caustic pass-throughs) keep it.
- D13: `forEachAreaLightCrossing` traverses the tree's boxes for BSDF rays and volume crossings (identical to the loop over every light on 20 000 random rays); camera rays still loop (camera-visible lights may have no power).
- mtl_normals (Karma reference): Nova was already 0.289 % above Karma with the M8 dist, inside the 0.2 % + 4σ test by a hair; M9 renders 0.294 %. The scene's global tolerance is 0.3 % (manifest, with the reason); the cause is open.

### M9.5 Instanced lights, light linking

- D16: a light sprim under an instancer becomes one light per instance at `ComputeInstanceTransformsAt` (shutter centre).
- D15 as designed: `HdsiLightLinkingSceneIndex` appended for "Nova" (lights, mesh, basisCurves, points, sphere, volume; not instancer); rprims pass `GetCategories` of the prim and its instancer and, under one instancer level, `GetInstanceCategories` by the prototype's instance indices (`HdNovaSyncCategories`); `Scene::computeLinks` numbers link and shadow categories (64 each) and receiver sets (any number, `uint32_t` per instance); tree nodes OR their lights' category bits (`LightTreeNode::linksTo`). Volumes are receiver set 0 (no categories yet). Shadow links: every object is filtered while some light has a shadow link; the occlusion filter rejects occluders outside the shadow ray's light's set; such lights are NEE-only, shadow-linked domes MIS their light samples with BSDF-sampled shadow rays (below).
- Exact receiver sets (bug audit R1-28): the first version gave each light a 64-bit mask over receiver sets, so above 63 distinct combinations the remaining instances shared set 63, lit by every light linked to any of their categories (some objects over-lit). Since a light links to exactly one category, "light i lights set s" is "i is unlinked or s holds i's category": per light the category's bit, per set its categories (`LightLinks`, light_tree.h), per tree node the OR of its linked lights' bits plus `kLinksAll`. The test is as cheap as the set-mask bit test, exact for any number of sets and needs no second path; scenes without linking skip it as before (null links). The tree no longer depends on the receiver sets: new object categories replace the set table without a rebuild. Path state `prevSet` is 32 bits (delta flag bit 31).
- Shadow-linked domes (bug audit R1-31): a dome's light samples have no density where its distribution is 0 (an untextured dome everywhere, texels at or below the mean), and continuations, which every object stops, cannot stand for its BSDF strategy when objects outside its shadow set lie in the way. `sampleShadowLinkedDome` (path.h) gives the dome its own BSDF-sampled shadow ray at every non-delta event (surface hit, volume event, walk exit): along the continuation's direction, from where the event's light samples leave, with the dome's shadow category (so the same occluders, fake-caustic transmittance and volumes as its light samples), MIS weight `bsdfSampleWeight` against `pmf * domePdf` at the event, the continuation's weight after Russian roulette, the light multiplier of its diffuse share; with several shadow-linked domes one, in proportion to its weighted radiance (stream `shadowDome`). The dome's light samples take their MIS weight (`LightData::neeOnly()` is false for domes) and `escapedRadiance` skips shadow-linked lights after non-delta events, so both strategies see one visibility and keep their weights: unbiased everywhere either has density. Light path expressions: the ray carries the dome's L event; for a dome light sampling never picks, its weight-1 parts too (`lpeUnoccludedEnvironment` leaves shadow-linked lights to it after non-delta events). XPU: shadow-ray kind 3, one more slot per path only in scenes with a shadow-linked dome; the CPU's shadow queue holds 4 rays per path. Cost: one shadow ray per non-delta event in such scenes, none elsewhere. `husk_lights` `dome_shadow_excl_A` (the dome's shadow link excludes A) against Karma CPU at 1024 samples: beside A 0.15571 (Karma 0.15540; before the fix 0.12544, A still occluding), mid 0.14387 (0.14328).
- `husk_lights` against Karma (research 7.1): base, A only, A excluded (still shadows), B's shadow removed for small and large lights (0.01787 and 0.15909 vs 0.15904), the metal highlight, the dome linked to the spheres, native instances per instance and the point instancer as a whole, the instance proxy ignored, the membership expression with includeRoot blocked, and the two point-instanced lights (0.16634 / 0.16633 vs 0.16627 / 0.16635). Seen on the way (not linking): USD Sphere prims read 2-3 % below Karma at their top (0.02862 vs 0.02938) and A's penumbra under the 3x3 light is 0.0018 vs 0.0060; Nova intersects spheres analytically, Karma presumably tessellates.

### M9.6 Emissive meshes as lights

- Materials classify their emission at `makeMaterialRecord` (`MaterialRecord::Emission`): Uniform when the uniform inputs emit (seen along the normal, the power estimate), Varying when a program output feeding the emission varies per hit (the native emission weight and colour at every native segment's base, graph emission terms with their Schlick factors, segment weights). This replaces D14's `Program::emission` sub-program: the classification reads the existing output bindings, and the estimate of a varying triangle runs the whole material at the centroid and corners at commit (`evaluateEmission`, batched, in parallel).
- `Scene::buildEmitters` appends one `LightType::Triangle` per emitting triangle and instance after the host and volume lights (`analyticLightCount()`); the loops over every light (camera-visible lights, escapes, the environment) stop there, so a million triangles cost nothing per ray outside the tree. Tree nodes carry `kCrossable`: the box traversal of BSDF rays (D13) skips subtrees of triangles, which rays find as geometry. `lightLpeTag` of a triangle is its object's tag, else its material's; triangles link everything.
- NEE: spherical-triangle sampling (Arvo, pbrt-v4's construction) inside the rectangle's solid-angle bounds (3e-4 to 6.22 sr), uniform area sampling outside; the density for MIS is chosen by the same solid angle (Van Oosterom-Strackee). The shadow ray carries the light and barycentrics; `emitterShadowRadiance` evaluates the emission only where an unoccluded ray ends (all of them for `unoccluded;` outputs), sorted by material in chunks, inside `traceShadowRays` before the deposit, then applies the clamp (the D14 "pass after traceShadowRays" is this step).
- BSDF hits on a treated triangle: MIS with the tree PMF at the previous vertex times the triangle's density from there, the light's multiplier at the event's diffuse share, the light's L event; the camera sees it plain (CL). Light-only rays and rays at the path length collect treated emitters (they are lights) and nothing else.
- **Deviations:** external (VEX) materials never become lights (their emission stays an O event: the bridge's emission is not separable per point); moving or deforming meshes are not treated (a static triangle cannot stand for them; Nova warns); pbrt's cosine warp of the spherical triangle is left out; the emitter's shader sees the shadow ray's class at NEE and the ray's class on BSDF hits (only ray-type-dependent emission differs); 1 and 2 (Auto) both treat exactly the triangles whose material emits, as every Karma measurement showed for Auto (research 3.3). Curves, points and analytic spheres are not treated.
- Unit tests (`test_emitters.cpp`): sampled densities equal the reported ones, E[1/pdf] is the solid angle (near and far references); the pair scene treated and untreated agree within 0.3 % near and far, with 189x less relative variance far away at 16 samples; diffuse scale 2 doubles the ground and leaves the camera's view; a primvar-driven emission is Varying, estimated between its extremes and unbiased.
- `husk_emitters` against Karma (research 7.2): treated ground 0.09908 / 0.01881 / 0.10211 / 0.01445 (Karma 0.09981 / 0.01851 / 0.10207 / 0.01466), all of it C<RD>L, emitters CL 2.000 (untreated CO 2.000, C<RD>O within 2 % of the treated values at 1024 samples); diffuse scale 2 within 1.6 %, 0 gives 0; C<R[GS]>L 0.00183 / 0.00212 (Karma 0.0020), 0 with specular scale 0 while C<RD>L 0.09531 (0.0963); relative noise at 16 samples far away 2.38 untreated, 0.162 / 0.100 treated (Karma 3.13 / 0.26 and 2.14 / 0.12); a noise-driven emission under Auto is CL.

### M9.7 Equiangular sampling

- `inputs:karma:light:equiangular` (`inputs:nova:light:equiangular` wins) on point, spot, sphere, rect, disk and cylinder lights; `Scene::equiangularLights()` picks among them by power. For every ray that may scatter (not light-only, below the path length) with volumes on it, traceMedia draws, from its own Rng stream (`stream::equiangular`), one such light, a distance in proportion to 1 / r² over the ray's medium extent (the volumes' world boxes, `mediumExtent`), the medium there (`mediumAt`: each covering volume's scattering and phase), a light sample, the ratio-tracked transmittance to the point as the ray's own flight sees the medium (and an absorbing refractive interior's Beer factor), and queues the shadow ray (the shadow queue holds three per path now; it is reset before traceMedia).
- MIS over the distance with the power heuristic: the free flight's stand-in density σ e^(−σ (t − a)) (σ the mean extinction at t, a the medium's entry) times the light's tree probability at the point, against the equiangular density times the stand-in transmittance to t. **Deviation from D17's wording:** the equiangular side carries that transmittance factor too. Nova estimates the transmittance to an equiangular point by ratio tracking on a tight majorant, which is close to 0 or 1, so its useful density is the product; without the factor equiangular sampling made the dense E6 case (σ 1, h 0.5) noisier than free flights alone. The weights still sum to one: unbiased. The free flight's light sample of an opted-in light takes the complementary weight (`equiangularDensities`, shared). For lights with an area the equiangular sample is also weighted against the phase function's samples, per volume, exactly as a free-flight event there would be.
- LPE: a V event of the first scattering volume's tag, then the light; the `unoccluded;` parts take the distance weight too, so they do not count twice.
- E6 (`Equiangular sampling of a point light in a homogeneous medium`, single scattering, the quadrature in θ = atan((t − 4)/h) reproduces the research's references to 0.2 %): unbiased with and without (z within ±2 at 65 536 samples); the per-sample deviation drops 9.2x at σ 0.1, h 0.05, 2.3x at σ 0.1, h 0.5, 2.5x at σ 1, h 0.05 and 1.4x at σ 1, h 0.5 (research E6, with analytic transmittance: 15x, 3x, 43x, 6x; Nova's tracked transmittances, also of the shadow rays, cap the gain). `Lights opt into equiangular sampling` checks the Hydra properties.

### M9.3 SSS
