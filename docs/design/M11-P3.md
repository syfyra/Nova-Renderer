# M11 P3: Karma Sky Dome Light and Karma Physical Sky (sky mode)

Package P3 of [M11.md](M11.md): the Karma Sky Dome Light LOP (`karmaskydomelight`) and the sky mode of the Karma
Physical Sky LOP (`karmaphysicalsky`) render in Nova as in Karma, on CPU and XPU. Research:
[karma-lops.md](../research/m11/karma-lops.md) (K3, section 2 probes).

## 1. Result

- Hydra's generic `light` sprim is registered. A generic light that carries Karma's sky inputs (`solar_altitude`,
  `turbidity`, or the shader id `KMAskyDomeLight`) becomes a dome light whose texture is the sky, baked from the
  Hosek-Wilkie model exactly as Karma evaluates it. Every other generic light is ignored with one warning per prim.
- The bake goes through Nova's existing textured dome: importance sampling, MIS, the light tree, light linking,
  camera visibility (`karma:light:renderlightgeo`), LPE tags and Nova XPU are unchanged.
- Karma's sky is the CIE XYZ Hosek-Wilkie model. Measured against Karma, Nova's sky pixels match within 0.01 %
  away from the horizon, and the irradiance on a white Lambert ground matches the model's integral within 0.01 %.
  Karma itself reads 0.0 to 0.8 % above the model because its default MIS on the sky is biased high (section 2.4).
- The Physical Sky in sky mode is its sun DistantLight, which Nova already renders, plus this sky. Both were checked
  at solar altitudes 5, 30 and 60 degrees and turbidities 2, 3 and 6, on CPU and XPU (section 4).
- IPR: editing a sky input re-bakes the sky (about 50 ms at the default resolution). Edits to the transform,
  visibility or linking reuse the bake.

## 2. What Karma does (evidence)

### 2.1 The model

- **Binaries.** `libBRAY.dll` and `libTIL.dll` export `TIL_HosekSky::TILinitState`, `TILskyRadiance`,
  `TILsolarRadiance`, `TILintegrateSky` and `TILcreateSkyMap`, and also `BRAY_EnvLight::initSkylight`,
  `buildSkylightCdf` and `buildSkylightLumMap`. The light shader `KMAskyDomeLight` is fed with the parameters
  `solardir`, `coeffX`, `coeffY`, `coeffZ`, `radiance`, `horizon_blur`, `computegroundcolor` and `ground_color`.
  That is a Hosek-Wilkie state per CIE XYZ channel: nine distribution coefficients and one radiance each.
- **Measurement.** Karma CPU rendered every scene in `tests/husk/karma_sky/` with husk. The camera was
  camera-visible (`renderlightgeo`) and used a box filter of zero width, so each pixel holds the radiance in its
  center direction. A Python model built from the authors' published 1.4a CIE XYZ data reproduces those pixels.
  The model's XYZ is multiplied by 2 pi / 683, converted with the sRGB/Rec.709 (D65) matrix and multiplied by the
  light color. All pixels above 5 degrees of altitude agree with ratio 1.00000 +- 4e-6. That holds for 10 Sky Dome
  Light configurations and 9 Physical Sky configurations: altitudes 0 to 90, azimuths 0, 30, 90 and 200,
  turbidities 1 to 10, albedos 0, 0.5, 1 and colored, light color, intensity and exposure, and a rotated light.
  The model's RGB dataset is ruled out: it is off by up to 4 %.
- **Data license.** The data is the authors' BSD 3-Clause release. It is vendored unchanged in
  `third_party/hosek_wilkie` from the authors' site, not from Houdini's copy.

### 2.2 Conventions (each measured; probes in this package's scratch, values in `tests/test_sky.cpp`)

| Input | Karma |
|---|---|
| frame | the light's own frame, +Y up; the light transform rotates the sky |
| `solar_altitude` | degrees, clamped to [0, 90] (-10 renders as 0, 100 as 90) |
| `solar_azimuth` | degrees; the sun is at (cos a sin z, sin a, -cos a cos z): 0 points down -Z, 90 down +X, the same as the Physical Sky's sun light |
| `turbidity` | clamped to [1, 10], the data's range |
| `ground_albedo` | feeds the model per channel, R to X, G to Y and B to Z (no color conversion), each clamped to [0, 1] |
| `horizon_blur` | **degrees**. Below altitude h = min(blur, 1/64 radian) the sky takes the radiance it has at altitude h, blended into the ground color with smoothstep from altitude -blur up to h. Blur 0 gives a hard edge at the horizon. Default 0.5: a fade from -0.5 to +0.5 degrees |
| `ground_color` | the radiance below the horizon. Karma does not compute it from the albedo, even with `computegroundcolor` on (the default; the Sky Dome Light LOP authors 0, which is black) |
| `computegroundcolor` | 1 (schema default): `ground_color` is used as it is. 0: `ground_color` times the light color |
| `color`, `intensity`, `exposure` | multiply the sky (light color = color x intensity x 2^exposure) |
| `enableColorTemperature`, `normalize` | ignored; a sky with 4000 K, or with normalize on, renders like the default |
| rendering space | the sky's linear Rec.709 is converted to OCIO's `scene_linear`. The ground color is not converted |

### 2.3 The sun

The sky contains no solar disc. Karma's pixels at and around the sun direction equal the model, which has no
disc. The Physical Sky's sun is its own DistantLight: angle 0.51, normalize on, `renderlightgeo` on, and a color and
intensity the LOP computes from `karma/hoseksolarmap.rat` (via `TILsolarRadiance`). The LOP also computes the
sky's `ground_color` from its albedo and authors it. Nova reads the authored values, so the sun is never counted
twice. The visible disc matches Karma's within 0.1 % (ps_a5_t6, front view: Nova 3766, Karma 3770).

### 2.4 Absolute scale and Karma's bias

A white Lambert quad facing up, under the sky only, has radiance E / pi. Compared with the model's integral at
1024 samples per pixel, Karma reads high by:

- +0.2 to +0.8 % with its defaults (worst: altitude 5, turbidity 2, red channel +0.79 %);
- +0.00 to +0.14 % with `karma:light:misbias = -1`.

So Karma's default MIS on the sky is biased, as with textured domes (tests/reference/README.md). Nova matches the
integral within 0.01 % (pixel noise 0.03 %).

## 3. Implementation

| File | |
|---|---|
| `third_party/hosek_wilkie/` | the authors' `ArHosekSkyModelData_CIEXYZ.h` (1.4a, unchanged), LICENSE, README.nova.md with source and hashes |
| `core/lights/sky.{h,cpp}` | Nova's own code: `makeHosekSkyState` (quintic Bezier in the cube root of the elevation, linear in turbidity and albedo), `hosekSkyXYZ`, `KarmaSkyDesc`, `karmaSkyRadiance` (Karma's conventions, section 2.2), `bakeKarmaSky` (latlong in the dome mapping of `core/lights/lights.h`, rows in parallel) |
| `hydra/light.{h,cpp}` | `light` sprims: recognized as a Karma sky by `kma:light:shaderId` or the sky inputs (looked up as `inputs:<name>`, then `<name>`). The light becomes a Dome whose texture holds the final radiance (color, intensity and exposure are 1). Bake inputs are cached, so only sky input edits re-bake. Other generic lights warn once and are dropped |
| `hydra/renderDelegate.cpp` | `light` in the supported sprim types |
| `hydra/implicitSurfaceSceneIndexPlugin.cpp` | `light` among the light types of the light-linking scene index (the sky's `collection:lightLink`/`shadowLink`) |
| `tools/nova_cli` | registers Houdini's resource-only `usdKarma` schema plugin, so nova_cli knows `KarmaSkyDomeLight` as husk does |

**Resolution.** The default bake is 2048 x 1024 (0.18 degrees per texel). The per-light override is
`inputs:nova:light:skyResolution`, an int width (16 to 16384, height half of it). Sampling the bake bilinearly against
the exact model, for random directions outside the horizon fade, gives:

| Width | Max error | 99.9th percentile |
|---|---|---|
| 1024 | 0.27 % | 0.12 % |
| 2048 | 0.04 % | 0.03 % |

Cost at 2048 (Ryzen 9800X3D): bake 19 ms, the dome's sampling distribution 31 ms. At 1024: 6 and 8 ms. At 4096:
70 and 69 ms.

**Limit of the bake.** Inside the horizon fade (default 1 degree wide) the bilinear lookup interpolates linearly
between texel rows where Karma's blend is a smoothstep. In husk front views at 0.75 degrees per pixel, Nova differs
there by up to about 2 % of the horizon radiance (absolute 0.003 to 0.006). Outside the fade the agreement is
0.01 %. Higher `skyResolution` narrows it.

## 5. Open points

- The schema's IES, focus and cone inputs on `KarmaSkyDomeLight` mean nothing for a dome and are ignored. Neither
  Karma nor the LOP uses them for the sky.
- The Physical Sky's atmosphere mode (`KarmaSkyAtmosphere`) is P7.
- Not checked in the Solaris GUI viewport. The IPR path is covered by the Hydra test, which edits through
  UsdImaging's scene indices as Solaris does.
- The golden check gains one scene (karma_sky). The existing scenes are bit-identical.
