# M11 P9c: Path guiding (Karma Render Settings)

Package P9c of [M11.md](M11.md): the Karma Render Settings LOP's path guiding, `karma:global:guiding_enable`,
`guiding_samples`, `guiding_components`, `guiding_deterministic`, `guiding_clearbuffers` (inventory:
[karma-properties.md](../research/m11/karma-properties.md) section 1). P1 warned that guiding was unsupported; Nova
now guides on both devices and the warning is gone.

Guiding changes only the noise, never the converged image: "matching Karma" means the same means with guiding off
and on, a clear noise drop on indirect-heavy scenes, and controls that behave as Karma's.

## 1. Karma, measured

Karma CPU and XPU 22.0.466, isolated husk, the scenes of `tests/husk/karma_guiding/` (written by
`build_karma_guiding.py` from the real LOP): `room` is a closed grey room lit by a sun through a window (Karma's
default diffuse limit 1, so everything the camera sees off the sunlit floor patch is light bounced once off that
patch); `fog` is the same room filled with a Karma Fog Box (volume limit 1). 32 Path Traced samples, uniform oracle,
no colour limits, 8 seeds (`karma:global:randomseed`). Noise: the median over 8 x 8 pixel blocks of the relative
variance of the block means across the seeds ("block"), the same per pixel ("pixel").

| Case | Mean | Block noise | Pixel noise |
|---|---|---|---|
| room, guiding off | 0.02659 | 1.505e-2 | 1.905 |
| room, on (training throughout) | 0.02660 | 1.928e-2 | 1.328 |
| room, 8 training samples | 0.02658 | 1.902e-2 | 1.350 |
| room, 8 training samples, clearbuffers | 0.02660 | 2.777e-2 | 1.906 |
| room, components `reflect` | 0.02659 | 1.505e-2 | 1.905 |
| room, deterministic | 0.02660 | 1.746e-2 | 1.360 |
| fog, off | 0.01154 | 8.142e-3 | 0.5422 |
| fog, on | 0.01152 | 7.075e-3 | 0.4491 |
| fog, components `diffuse` | 0.01154 | 8.849e-3 | 0.5594 |

What this says about Karma:

- **The mean does not move** (within 0.2 %), as expected of an unbiased guide.
- **Noise**: per pixel -30 % in the room, -17 % in the fog. The block noise of the room *rises* 28 %: the learned
  distribution differs from run to run and shifts whole regions together (low-frequency noise), while per-pixel
  noise drops. Karma's gains at 32 samples are moderate.
- **Training samples** (`guiding_samples` 8, then the frozen field): as good as training throughout here.
- **clearbuffers**: the render still takes 32 samples in all; the 8 training samples are dropped from the image,
  which then holds 24 (pixel noise x1.41 of the 8-sample training case, 32/24 = 1.33 expected from the count alone).
  With training throughout (0) nothing is ever dropped.
- **Components**: with only `reflect` listed, a diffuse room renders *bit-identically* to guiding off (same random
  numbers); with volumes left out of the list, the fog loses its gain (as off).
- **Deterministic**: two renders with one seed are identical; without it they differ (max |diff| 0.053 on 0.027
  mean), Open PGL's multithreaded training being order-dependent.
- **Karma XPU ignores guiding**: on and off render identical images on both scenes (no message).
- Cost: Karma CPU's guided render took 1.04x (room) and 1.10x (fog) the time (noisy timings, a build ran alongside).

## 2. Design decision: Nova's own guiding field (option 2)

Karma CPU uses Intel Open PGL. Nova does not:

- Open PGL needs oneTBB; Houdini loads its own TBB into the process, and Nova's dependencies come from `$HFS` or
  vcpkg (Embree is built with its internal tasking for exactly this reason, memory nova-project). A second TBB, or
  an Open PGL built against Houdini's TBB headers at a matching ABI, is a real risk for a plugin that runs inside
  Houdini.
- Open PGL is CPU-only: its distributions cannot be sampled inside Nova XPU's CUDA kernels, so XPU would differ, and
  M11's rule is "CPU and XPU identical, integrator code in shared NOVA_INLINE headers". (Karma XPU simply ignores
  guiding; Nova XPU should not have to.)
- Its training is not deterministic by default, and its deterministic mode costs extra (Karma's help says so).

So Nova implements **Practical Path Guiding** (Müller, Gross, Novák, "Practical Path Guiding for Efficient
Light-Transport Simulation", EGSR 2017; the SD-tree), written from the paper: a binary spatial tree over the scene's
bounds whose leaves each hold a directional quadtree over the sphere, trained between progressive passes in
iterations of doubling sample counts. Plain arrays of nodes, so the same NOVA_INLINE code samples and evaluates
them on the CPU and in the CUDA kernels; training is a splat of one integer per path vertex. No third-party code,
no new dependency.

## 3. The guiding field (core/integrator/guiding.h, guide_field.{h,cpp})

**Spatial tree.** The scene's bounds made a cube (PPG); node children split their parent at the middle of the axis
x, y, z in turn by depth. A leaf holds a sampling quadtree (read by the shading) and a building quadtree (written
by the training). After iteration k a leaf that took more than c sqrt(2^k) records (c = `kSpatialThreshold`, 2^k
the iteration's samples per pixel) splits, recursively with halved counts; the children start from the parent's
distribution. A node budget (`kMaxNodes`, 2^20 quadtree nodes) stops splitting. PPG's c is 12000; measured on the
160 x 120 room at 32 samples (pixel noise against guiding off): c 4000 x0.92, 2000 x0.83, 1000 x0.77, 500 x0.75,
250 x0.98 (too few records per leaf). Nova takes 1000: the records per iteration grow with the image, so larger
images get finer trees (1920 x 1080 at 32 samples: about 10 000 leaves).

**Directional quadtree.** The sphere mapped to the unit square by (cos theta, phi), area-preserving, so a
quadrant's density on the sphere is its share of the energy times 4^depth / (4 pi). Each node holds its four
quadrants' energies and children. Sampling descends by the energies with the stratified pair warped hierarchically
(the pair stays stratified); the density is the product of 4 x share along the path. Refinement (PPG): a quadrant
holding more than `kDirThreshold` (1 %) of the leaf's energy is subdivided (up to depth 20), recursively within one
iteration, a leaf quadrant's energy spread evenly over its new children.

**Carried energy (a deviation from PPG).** PPG starts each iteration's building tree at zero, so every field is
learned from one iteration's samples alone. Nova's new building tree starts with half of the energy learned so
far (`kCarry`; the next iteration is twice as long, so every sample weighs the same; a split leaf's children half
again). At the sample counts Karma renders by default this matters: on the room the pixel noise went from x0.96 to
x0.92 of guiding off at c 4000. The carried sums are integers like the records (deterministic).

**Training records.** At a guided vertex whose continuation is a full (not light-only, not delta) ray, the path
stores the building quadtree slot of the sampled direction (at the vertex position jittered within its spatial
cell: PPG's stochastic box filter), 1 / (throughput after the vertex x its density), and, once the vertex's own
light samples are in, a snapshot of the path's radiance (`kVertices` vertices per path). When the path ends, each
vertex's incident radiance over its density, luminance of (radiance - snapshot) / (throughput x density), is added
to its slot. All of a path's light after the vertex, whatever strategy found it (BSDF rays, light samples at later
vertices, emission, volumes), is the radiance arriving along the sampled direction.

**Deterministic always.** Records are added as 64-bit fixed-point integers (`kRecordScale` 2^20, stochastically
rounded with a per-vertex hash, values capped at `kRecordMax`, divided by the iteration's sample count against
overflow), so the sums do not depend on the order in which threads or GPU warps add them, and the refinement on the
host is sequential per leaf. The field, and so the image, is the same for the same seed whatever the thread count
and from run to run, on each device. Karma's `guiding_deterministic` therefore changes nothing in Nova (documented,
not warned: Nova always behaves as Karma's deterministic mode, at no extra cost).

**Iterations.** Iteration k covers 2^k samples per pixel (passes 1, 2, 4, 8, ...); at its end the building trees
become the sampling trees and the structure is refined. Before the first iteration ends there is no field: guided
vertices only record (the image is that of guiding off). `guiding_samples` N > 0 stops training once N samples are
done (the last iteration may be shorter; with the carried energy it only adds). 0 trains throughout. Iterations
growing by 1.5 or 1.25 instead of 2 measured no better (x0.76, x0.78).

**Sample numbers (no reuse).** A field must not be used with the random numbers it was trained on: paths drawn
again with them correlate with it. Measured: rendering 32 samples after `guiding_clearbuffers` dropped 32 training
samples, with the pixels' sample numbers restarting at 0, came out 2.3 % dark (7 sigma); a field memorises the
directions its own training paths found. So a film that restarts while its field lives (clearbuffers, an IPR
restart that keeps the field) continues the sample numbers after the ones it rendered (`Film::sampleOffset`); the
mean then agrees (0.02578 +- 0.00003 against 0.02576 +- 0.00007). Training throughout uses each field only on
later samples than it learned from.

## 4. Guided sampling (one-sample MIS, no bias)

**Surfaces.** A lobe is guided when its component is listed (`guiding_components`: diffuse lobes and sheen
`diffuse`, conductors `reflect`, coats `coat`, dielectrics `reflect` and/or `refract` by the sides they scatter to,
translucency and thin-walled SSS `sss`; delta lobes never) and its event class still has room under its ray limit
(a light-only continuation gains nothing from the guide). The closure's lobe choice stays as it is; a guided lobe
j, picked with probability p_j, takes its direction from the guide with probability alpha (0.5, PPG's BSDF sampling
fraction) and from its own sampling otherwise. The density of a direction is then

  p(w) = p_bsdf(w) + alpha (P_G p_guide(w) - sum over guided j of p_j pdf_j(w)),  P_G = sum over guided j of p_j

(a mixture, so > 0 wherever the BSDF's own density is), the weight f cos / p(w) for every non-delta sample whatever
strategy drew it (one-sample MIS with the balance heuristic), and the same p(w) is the continuation's density in the
light sample's MIS weight at this vertex and in the BSDF-hit MIS at the next (`bsdfPdf`). Delta lobes and unguided
lobes keep their probabilities, so mirrors and glass sample as before. The event class of a guided sample is the
picked lobe's (as for any mixture sample), its LPE class by the lobes' values (lpe_path.h). VEX (external) closures
are not guided.

**Volumes** (`volume`): the phase function mixed with the guide, p = (1 - alpha) phase + alpha p_guide, weight
phase / p. **SSS** (`sss`): the cosine continuation at a random walk's exit, mixed the same way.

**Random numbers.** The guide-or-lobe decision has its own sampler stream (`stream::guide`), the record jitter and
rounding their own hashes; the closure's streams are untouched. Without a guided lobe at a vertex nothing is drawn,
so a scene with no guided component renders bit-identically to guiding off (as Karma's `reflect`-only room does).

## 5. Integration

- `RenderSettings::guiding` (`GuidingSettings`); `Renderer` owns the `PathGuide`, hands each pass its `GuideView`
  (sampling and/or training), counts samples, ends iterations after passes, and resets the field when the scene's
  version or the guiding settings change (IPR edits retrain; camera moves keep the field).
- CPU: `renderWave` takes the view; the snapshot runs at the start of each bounce, the records at the wave's end
  (relaxed atomic adds on the host arrays).
- XPU: `GpuScene::guide` (device copies of the arrays, uploaded when the field's generation changes, sums cleared),
  the per-path records in `GpuWave`, kernels `novaGuideSnap` (each bounce) and `novaGuideRecord` (the wave's end,
  integer `atomicAdd`); at an iteration's end the host adds the device sums to its own and refines.
- clearbuffers: when training ends at N < the render's samples, the film is cleared (its sample numbers continue,
  section 3) and the render continues to its total (N training samples plus the rest, as Karma); the guide is
  kept. With training throughout nothing is dropped; N at or above the render's samples drops nothing either (the
  render is all training).
- The pixel oracle and proxy passes work as before (passes with fewer pixels train with fewer records; proxy
  passes count no samples). Bakes are not guided.
- GPU registers (ptxas, sm_120): `novaShade` 243 -> 255 (4 bytes spilled), `novaVolumes` 255 -> 255 (spills 56 ->
  88 bytes), `novaWalkExit` 199 -> 202; the guide's descents and records are `__noinline__` helpers.

## 6. Settings (the P1 rule: Nova's if authored, else Karma's, else the default)

| Nova | Karma | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `nova:guiding` | `karma:global:guiding_enable` | off |
| `nova:guidingSamples` | `karma:global:guiding_samples` (primary samples: x `varianceaa_maxsamples` in Variance/Distributed modes, P1's mapping) | 0 |
| `nova:guidingComponents` | `karma:global:guiding_components` | `diffuse volume sss` |
| (none: always deterministic) | `karma:global:guiding_deterministic` | - |
| `nova:guidingClearBuffers` | `karma:global:guiding_clearbuffers` | off |

Unknown component names warn once.
