# M11 P7: Karma Sky Atmosphere and Karma Physical Sky (atmosphere mode)

Package P7 of [M11](M11.md): the Karma Sky Atmosphere LOP (`karmaskyatmosphere`) and the atmosphere mode of the
Karma Physical Sky LOP (`karmaphysicalsky`, Sky Mode "Karma Sky Atmosphere") render in Nova as in Karma, on CPU and
XPU. Research: [karma-lops.md](../research/m11/karma-lops.md) (K9).

## 1. Result

- Houdini's imaging adapter (`HD_KarmaAtmosphereAdapter` in `USD_HD.dll`, a gprim adapter) hands the
  `KarmaSkyAtmosphere` prim to delegates as an rprim of type **`karmaSkyAtmosphere`** (the token is in
  `BRAY_HdKarma.dll`; HdHDebug does not list it because it does not support the type, so the adapter skips it).
  Nova registers the type (`hydra/atmosphere.{h,cpp}`).
- Nova renders it as a **true heterogeneous medium in its path tracer** with Karma's model (section 2): exponential
  Rayleigh and Mie layers, an ozone tent, Rayleigh's and Henyey-Greenstein's phase functions, a planet with an opaque
  ground lit by the distant lights. Camera rays, secondary rays and shadow rays all see it, so multiple scattering
  (Karma's volume limit semantics), aerial perspective on geometry, volumetric shadows of objects, the sun's
  attenuation on surfaces, local lights scattering in the air and clouds (grid volumes) inside the atmosphere all
  follow from the integrator, on CPU and XPU alike.
- The transmittance is **computed, not estimated**: the density is analytic, so the optical depth of any ray
  segment comes from 4-point Gauss-Legendre quadrature between the segment's crossings of a few altitude levels
  (relative error below 2e-5, against a 400 000-step quadrature). The in-scattering distance is sampled once per
  ray segment from a piecewise-exponential proposal of sigma_s T, with the exact weight. No marching, no tables:
  Nova's atmosphere is exact up to the quadrature, where Karma's transmittance is biased by a few percent
  (section 2.4).

## 2. What Karma does (evidence)

Probes: isolated hython (`run_hython.ps1`) built scenes with the actual LOPs, isolated husk rendered them with Karma
CPU 22.0.466 (pixel-centre sampling, box filter of zero width, 256-2048 samples). An independent numerical model
(double precision, fine midpoint quadrature of the single-scattering integral and of the sun's optical depth) was
fitted to Karma's pixels. Scratch: `...\scratchpad\m11_p7` (probe generators `make_probes.py`, `gen_batch*.py`, the
model `atmo_ref.py`, comparisons `cmp_views.py`, `fit_*.py`, `aerial.py`, `top.py`, `patch*.py`).

- `libBRAY.dll` reads the prim's primvars by name (`rayleighcoeff`, `rayleighdist`, `miescattercoeff`,

  more than 1 atmospherics: ... will be ignored". (Symbol names only.)
- The LOP authors the coefficients per metre: the UI's colour times its scale (Rayleigh 0.2/0.4/0.9 x 3e-5 =
  (6, 12, 27)e-6, Mie scattering 0.8 x 5e-6, Mie absorption 0.9 x 5e-6, ozone (0.325, 0.94, 0.043) x 2e-6),

  ground visibility `-primary`, `karma:object:volumelimit` 1 ("Multi Scatter Limit"), LUT 256 x 256, steps
  250-2500 m. The Physical Sky in atmosphere mode authors the same prim (with `ociofrom` `lin_rec709`) and a sun
  DistantLight of colour 1, intensity 1, angle 0.51, normalized.

### 2.2 The model (measured)

| Item | Karma |
|---|---|
| densities | Rayleigh e^(-h/H_R), Mie e^(-h/H_M), ozone a tent max(0, 1 - \|h - altitude\| / (width / 2)) (the width is the full width), h above the sea-level sphere |
| coefficients | Rayleigh scatters only; Mie extinction = scattering + absorption; ozone absorbs |
| phase functions | Rayleigh 3/(16 pi)(1 + cos^2); Mie Henyey-Greenstein with `miephase` (checked at g = 0.8, 0.5, 0, -0.5 and 1.5-12 degrees from the sun: no angular trend) |
| sun | a DistantLight; irradiance as for any distant light, attenuated by the atmosphere on surfaces, in the visible disc and for the scattering points; the planet shadows it (twilight renders with the sun 2 and 6 degrees below the horizon) |
| lights | every light scatters in the atmosphere (a strong sphere light inside it makes a glow) |
| top | 7 scale heights of the thicker exponential layer (56 km by default; 112 km with H_R = 16 km), or the top of the ozone layer if higher: a camera at 57.9 km sees nothing above it |
| below sea level | vacuum: `groundoffset` lowers the ground below the medium (an offset of 500 m renders like 0 from 100 m) |
| rays below the horizon | with the ground invisible to the camera (`-primary`) a camera ray's medium ends at the ground, nothing is behind it (`renderbackside` 1: the ray continues through the planet to the far side's atmosphere) |
| ground | an opaque sphere (sea level minus `groundoffset`), Lambert with `groundalbedo`, lit only by the distant lights through the atmosphere: no sky light (the ground at volume limit 1 equals volume limit 0 within 0.5 %), shadowed by objects only with `groundreceiveshadow`; visible per ray class through `groundvisibility` (a rendervisibility expression) |
| multiple scattering | the volume limit of `karma:object:volumelimit`; limit 1 adds 20-60 % to single scattering, limit 4 25-80 % |
| volumetric shadows | objects shadow the air (an occluder darkens the sky behind it to 0.3-0.5) |
| alpha | 1 - mean transmittance of the camera segment (the default atmosphere within 0.1 %; a Mie-only atmosphere reads 43 % higher, not understood) |
| `ociofrom` | the coefficients are converted from that colour space to the rendering space (ACEScg: red x 0.6) |
| `ignorecameraclip` | camera rays that miss see the atmosphere past the far clipping plane |
| transform | rotations and scales of the prim apply to the planet (the centre as a vector, the sea level scaled); its translation does not move the planet |
| `enableatmosphere` 0 | nothing (the sun unattenuated) |

### 2.3 Karma's bias

- **Short segments**: Karma marches with steps of 250-2500 m; a camera ray that meets the ground after 120 m gets
  half the in-scattering and opacity (0.0014 instead of 0.0028); with 10x finer steps it matches the model. Distant
  walls: black walls at 1-10 km match within 1-4 %, at 33 km Karma is 15 % low.
- **Transmittance**: Karma's optical depths are 3-7 % too large: the visible sun disc at 30 degrees is 1.0/1.4/2.5 %
  dark (R/G/B), at 5 degrees 3.4/4.5/7.4 %; the visible ground (direct sun) 1.7/2.2/3.4 % at 30 degrees; the sky's
  single scattering 0.2/0.8/2.4 % (red/green/blue; the blue deficit doubles with twice the Rayleigh coefficient and
  vanishes in an optically thin atmosphere, unit scale 1: 0.998/0.999/0.999). It does not depend on the step size;
  a 32 x 32 table changes the sky by about 1 %.
- Nova computes the transmittance (1e-5) and samples the in-scattering without bias, so Nova is that much brighter
  than Karma where the bias applies (section 5).

### 3.1 Transport

- **Segments.** For a ray, the medium runs from its origin (or its entry into the top sphere) to its end: a surface
  hit, the top, or the ground (when the ray sees the ground, or meets it without `renderbackside`). The segment is
  split where it crosses the altitude levels: sea level, every 2 Mie scale heights up to 8 of them, every 2 Rayleigh
  scale heights above, the ozone tent's corners (7 levels by default). Inside a band the exponentials change by at
  most about e^2 and the tent is linear, where 4-point Gauss-Legendre is exact to 1e-5.
- **Transmittance** (shadow rays, light-only rays, the pass-through weight, alpha): exp(-optical depth), times the
  grid volumes' ratio-tracked transmittance; zero where a visible ground blocks (the planet's shadow).
- **In-scattering.** A ray that may scatter decides once per segment: with probability P it scatters in the
  atmosphere at a distance drawn from the proposal (per band exponential in t, fitted to the throughput-weighted
  sigma_s T at the band's ends, bands by their mass), else it passes with weight T(end) / (1 - P). P is 1 when
  nothing lies behind (an escaping ray with no light at infinity in its direction), the segment's opacity when a
  surface, an environment, crossed lights or the ground lie behind, a tenth of it before a sun disc. The event's
  weight is sigma_s T / (P q(t)) with sigma_s = Rayleigh + Mie; it scatters by both at once
  (`PhaseFunction::kSky`): the light sample and the continuation use the mixture of Rayleigh's and Mie's phase
  functions weighted by their throughput-weighted shares as the density, and each channel is weighted by its own
  mixture over that density (`PhaseFunction::weight`). Picking one of the two per event instead made the red
  channel near the sun twice as noisy (Mie is a small share there but dominates the light). With grid
  volumes (clouds) their tracker runs up to the atmosphere's distance; their event wins and is divided by the
  probability S(t) = 1 - P Q(t) that the atmosphere's distance lay behind it, as are crossed lights and collected
  emission (one point process with at most one point: unbiased).
- **Events** are volume events of the atmosphere's record: phase function, light sample, continuation, the volume
  limit (`karma:object:volumelimit`, default 1), LPE tag (`karma:object:lpetag`) as for any volume.
- **The ground**: where a flight ends on it, its radiance albedo / pi sum E cos T_atm over the distant lights is
  added (with `groundreceiveshadow` through shadow rays), and nothing behind it is reached. An `O` event in light
  path expressions.

## 4. Decisions

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Medium or tables | **The medium.** Karma path-traces the atmosphere (volume limits, volumetric shadows, every light); precomputed scattering tables (Bruneton) would match only the default case. |
| A2 | Unbiased tracking or computed transmittance | **Computed.** Ratio tracking of a thin medium returns 0 or 1-like weights: direct sunlight on every surface would be noisy, and delta tracking's escapes make sky pixels 4x noisier than Karma's. The quadrature's error (2e-5) is far below Karma's bias. |
| A3 | The ground | **Karma's: direct distant light only**, opaque, by ray class; no BSDF continuation. |
| A4 | More than one atmosphere | The first visible one renders; the others warn (as Karma). |
| A5 | Ignored | `lutdim`, `stepsizemin`, `stepsizemax` (Nova does not march or tabulate), `solidalphainipr` (Karma's IPR alpha). Documented, no warning. |

The pattern is Karma's transmittance bias (section 2.3): it grows with the blue channel and the optical depth, and
the multiple-scattering scenes show the same ratios as single scattering, so Nova's volume-limit semantics match
Karma's. Against the model (independent quadrature): the zenith views' blocks at limit 0 within 2e-5 (sun 30 and
5), the visible ground within 3e-4, the sun disc 10314/7210/4293 against 10310/7207/4291. Alpha within 0.01 of
Karma's in every compared block (not in the Mie-heavy `sa_params`, whose alpha Karma computes differently).

**Noise** (per-pixel relative deviation from a converged image, 64 x 64 views at 256 samples): single scattering
Nova 0.01 % (sun 30) to 0.05 % (sun 5) rms against the model (the in-scattering distance is a stratified Sobol
dimension and the transmittance is computed), Karma 2.2 %; limit 1, zenith view Nova 1.1/1.1/1.5 %, Karma 2.5/2.5/2.8 %; horizontal view Nova 1.2/1.1/1.4 %,
Karma 3.1/2.9/3.0 %. Choosing Rayleigh or Mie per event instead of the mixture had left Nova's red channel at 5.5 %.

**Time** (`karma_atmosphere` at 576 x 384, 256 samples, husk wall time, Ryzen 9800X3D / RTX 5080): Karma CPU 89 s,
Nova CPU 44 s, Nova XPU 6.3 s. The 64 x 64 views of the check take 2-5 s per view in both renderers.

**Before P7** Nova ignored the prim (no `karmaSkyAtmosphere` rprim): a black sky and an unattenuated sun.

## 7. Open points

- Karma's atmospheric continuations do not pick up the emission or the reflected light of other objects (its
  help); Nova's do (physically complete; objects are tiny against the atmosphere, so the difference is small).
- The ground is lit by distant lights only (Karma, measured with the sun; other light types were not measured on
  the ground). Light linking of the atmosphere prim (Karma's tip to link it to the sun) is not read: every light
  scatters in it.
- Karma's alpha of Mie-heavy atmospheres (43 % above 1 - mean transmittance for a Mie-only one) is not understood
  and not matched.
- `solidalphainipr` (alpha 1 in Karma's IPR) is ignored: Nova's viewport shows the atmosphere's alpha.
- nova_cli renders no atmosphere (Houdini's adapter lives in `USD_HD.dll`, which only husk and Houdini load).
- A viewport check with the LOP live in an own Houdini instance was not run.
