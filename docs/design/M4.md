## 2. Scope

### 2.1 M4 delivers

- **Ray state in shading:**
  - globals RayType (Karma's measured classes: 0 camera, 1 diffuse, 2 glossy reflection, 3 glossy transmission, 4 SSS,
    5 volume, 6 shadow), RayDepth, HitDist, Backface;
  - `kma_rayswitch_*`, `kma_rayimport_*`, MaterialX `dot` nodes with `note = "ray:<var>"` (Karma's ray import
    convention), and through them `ND_kma_camerafalloff`.
- **Per-ray-type limits:**
  - per-path counters and the lobe event class (sheen counts as diffuse);
  - light-only rays past a limit (no emission, no shading);
  - fractional limits per path, per-object limits, thin wall at the refract limit.
- **Fake-caustic shadows** (`nova:object:fakeCaustics`, off by default): shadow rays and rays after a diffuse bounce
  cross the object's transmissive lobes with their transmittance.
- **Pattern nodes:**
  - Karma: `kma_ramp_*`/`kma_rampconst_*` (array spline with per-key basis), `kma_tangentrotate`, `kma_stderiv_*`,
    `kma_deriv_2d_*`, `kma_melanin`, `kma_voronoinoise2d/3d`, `kma_color_hextiling`/`kma_normalmap_hextiling`/
    `kma_hextiled_triplanar_*`;
  - MaterialX: `flake2d/3d`, `hextiledimage`/`hextilednormalmap`, the `karma_mikkt` input of the normal-map nodes,
    string/filename `geompropvalueuniform` (a texture per prim, face or instance).
- **Ray queries from shading:** `ND_ambientocclusion_float`, `kma_ao`, `kma_distance`, `kma_curvature`,
  `kma_roundededge`. A per-chunk batch goes through `IAccel`.
- **Generic pbrlib BSDF graphs:**
  - closure programs: layer, mix, add, multiply over all 1.39 leaves (a new Burley lobe, the generalized-Schlick
    color82 term), the EDFs, `surface`, `mix_surfaceshader` (native or graph segments), and the legacy
    `thin_film_bsdf` rewrite;
  - `kMaxLobes` 16;
  - glTF PBR, Disney principled, Lama and user graphs render.
- **Displacement:**
  - float/vector3/UsdPreviewSurface displacement in object space;
  - Karma-like dicing (raster edge length × quality) through OpenSubdiv Bfr with per-edge rates, polygon meshes
    included;
  - vertex-lane VM evaluation, smooth recomputed normals, motion steps, the triangle budget, IPR re-sync of the bound
    meshes.
- **Solaris:**
  - `soho/parameters/HdNovaRendererPlugin_{Global,Geometry,Viewport}.ds`, generated from one settings table: Nova
    tabs on the stock Render Settings and Render Geometry Settings LOPs, and in the viewport display options;
  - a Nova Render Settings LOP (HDA);
  - the Nova Material Builder (shelf tool + subnet, `kma` context, Nova tab mask);
  - hython tests in their own instance.

### 2.3 Deviations from PLAN.md (stated explicitly)

1. **`UsdRenderers.json` does not hold render-settings UI or AOV menus** (PLAN.md architecture, `houdini/package`).
   Houdini reads only delegate metadata from it. The UI lives in `soho/parameters/*.ds` and HDAs
   (render-settings-ui.md 1-2).
2. **No `nova_standard_surface` node and no Nova-only shader nodes** (PLAN.md "Material-Pipeline" 2 and 4). The uber
   shader of the builder is MaterialX Standard Surface/OpenPBR, native in Nova since M3. Nova's extensions (AO,
   curvature, ray switch, user data, ramp, triplanar) are authored as the Karma/MaterialX nodes Karma also renders
   (D1). A Nova-only nodedef would make the material unrenderable in Karma.
3. **The Nova Material Builder is a builder subnet created by a shelf tool, like Karma's** (no subnet HDA). Houdini's
   Karma Material Builder is a plain `subnet` with spare parameters; an HDA would break Houdini's builder tooling
   (Material Library LOP translation, tab masks).
4. **`nova:maxDepth` changes meaning** from the path length to a safety cap. Per-type limits (Q8) replace it as the
   look control; scenes that set only `nova:maxDepth` render with the default limits.
5. **Ray queries from shading** (AO, curvature, ...) are an addition the PLAN implies ("Nova-Erweiterungen ... AO,
   Curvature") but does not specify; they run per shading chunk on the CPU (GPU: a split kernel in M10).

## 3. Architecture changes

### 3.1 Path state and ray classes (M4.0, M4.1)

`WaveQueues` (SoA) gains per path:
- `uint8 rayType` (Karma's 0..6), and `uint8 counts[4]` (diffuse, reflect, refract, sss);
- `uint8 flags`: `lightOnly`, `afterDiffuse`, `thinWallPass`;
- `float limitDither` (a new Sobol stream `kLimit`, drawn at camera-ray generation; integer limit = floor(L) + (u < frac(L))).

`RayQueue` carries `rayType` to the opacity filter, so opacity programs see RayType (shadow 6 for shadow rays,
`kma_rayswitch` `shadow` input) and RayDepth.

`Lobe` gains `eventClass` (Diffuse/Reflect/Refract/SSS), returned by `sampleClosure`:
- diffuse: Lambert, QON, EON, Burley, Charlie, Zeltner, translucent;
- reflect: conductor and the reflection part of dielectric lobes (coat included);
- refract: the transmission part;
- SSS: the SSS fallback lobe.

`Lobe::diffuse` keeps driving light multipliers and ray cones.

### 3.2 Limits (M4.1)

Karma semantics K1–K5 (nodes-and-limits 1.3):
- Every scattering event has a class with its own counter. The limit is read from the **object at the scattering
  vertex** (`MeshDesc` fields, from `primvars:nova:object:*` or the RenderSettings defaults `nova:object:*`).
- An event within the limit makes a full ray. An event past it makes a **light-only** ray: pass A gathers crossed area
  lights and the environment, then the path ends. It never shades its hit and never adds surface emission.
- The vertex that issues a light-only ray still does full NEE.
- `thinWallAtRefractLimit`: refract events with L ≤ n_T < 2L pass straight through (−wo), keep the weight, and count.

### 3.3 Fake caustics (M4.2)

`nova:object:fakeCaustics` on the **caster**. The shadow stage has two phases:
1. `occluded()`, with fake-caustic meshes rejected by the hit filter (no cost in scenes without them).
2. For unoccluded rays only, an iterated closest-hit over fake meshes (≤ 8 crossings) multiplies the closure's
   `transmittance(−dir)`.

Path rays with `afterDiffuse` that hit a fake mesh:
- drop its non-diffuse reflection lobes;
- replace its transmission by one null lobe (direction −wo, weight = transmittance).

MIS stays consistent because both estimators use the same factor (nodes-and-limits 2.4).

### 3.4 Generic closures (M4.5)

Following bsdf-graphs 5:
- The VM computes every leaf parameter and every mix/multiply factor (outputs in an enlarged input block, 256 floats).
- A per-material POD `ClosureProgram` (post-order nodes, ≤ 48) holds the structure.
- `buildGraphClosure` runs two fixed loops: leaf albedos bottom-up, weights top-down with `1 − A_top` at ωo. It is
  DAG-safe and has no recursion.
- Native models stay native. Forced expansion of their own nodegraphs is the equivalence test. The leaf albedo helpers
  are shared (`leafAlbedo`).
- `ClosureList`: 16 lobes, 4 frames. On overflow the lobe with the smallest estimate is replaced, with one warning per
  material.

### 3.5 Ray queries (M4.4)

A `RayQuery` op family (AO, Distance, Curvature, RoundedEdge) is executed **per 32-lane chunk**:
- The executor runs the program up to the op for the whole chunk, builds `samples` rays per live lane (directions from
  a new Sobol stream `kShadingQuery`), and calls `IAccel` once (occluded or intersect).
- It reduces per lane, writes the result row and continues.
- These ops are never folded or hoisted, and are excluded from derivative clones.
- Queries never shade their hits. `scope:self` uses the hit filter.

### 3.6 Displacement (M4.6)

Following displacement.md 5:
- The translator compiles the `displacement` terminal into `Program::displacement` (object-space offset, DCE'd like
  `Program::opacity`). A constant zero offset means no displacement (Karma's builder always wires one).
- `HdNovaMesh::Sync`:
  - per-edge rates from the dicing view, rate = ceil(L_px · q), q = `primvars:nova:dicingQuality` (0.5) × the render
    setting `nova:dicingQualityScale`, an off-screen factor, `primvars:nova:dicingDepth` as the fixed mode and the
    no-camera fallback;
  - Bfr tessellation (bilinear for polygon meshes);
  - vertex-lane VM evaluation (owner-triangle rule, footprints from the tessellation edges);
  - offsets from step 0 on every step, area-weighted normals per step, the `nova:subdivMaxTriangles` budget.
- Spheres with displacement fall back to a tessellated mesh.
- IPR: a material → mesh index plus a path-list resync. Camera moves do not re-dice. The viewport dices after the
  first Execute knows the camera.

## 6. Notes as built

Notes on M4.0 as built:
- **Karma's nodes:** `HdNovaNodeDefDatabase` reads `<houdini>/karmaShaderNodes.json` (found two levels above the MaterialX library root, parsed with `pxr/base/js`) and adds a MaterialX nodedef for each of its 55 `kma_*` shader ids, named and categorized by the id, with the registry's input types and defaults (variadic inputs become arrays). Unimplemented ones lower to their defaults with one warning, like any unsupported node.
- **Ray state:** `RayQueue::rayType` carries `RayClass` (camera rays 0, continuation rays the sampled lobe's `eventClass`, shadow rays 6). `RayBatch` passes it, and the batch depth, to the opacity filter; occlusion queries are shadow rays. RayDepth is the bounce iteration. `Lobe::sss` marks the subsurface fallback lobes (Karma's SSS class); translucent lobes are SSS as well.
- **Translator:**
  - `kma_rayswitch_*` is a 7-way select on RayType.
  - `kma_rayimport_*` and `dot` nodes with a `ray:` note read direction, hitdist, hitN, hitNg, the two facing ratios (absolute), hitP, origin, isshadowray, level, backface and primid. Other variables pass the node's input through with one warning.
  - `kma_aov` 1.0 passes its input through.
  - A `dot` surface shader before the surface is followed like `surfacematerial`.
  - A displacement terminal warns only when it displaces (Karma's builder always wires a zero one).
- **Nodegraph output types:** the nodedef's output type wins over the graph's. Houdini's `kma_camerafalloff` graph declares its color output as float, and Karma follows the nodedef.
- **Tests:**
  - core: the ray-class probe (exact 0.1·(class+1) ratios for Lambert, mirror, rough metal, index-matched glass, sheen, thin-walled subsurface, direct view), RayDepth, HitDist 5.0, Backface, and shadow-ray opacity through a ray-type select (0.5 / 1 / 0);
  - Hydra: registry nodedefs, ray switch per class, ray imports and dot notes, camera falloff head-on/grazing, pass-throughs, displacement warnings.
  - **Coverage:** all materials of Houdini's `basic_materials.usd` (120) and `quick_materials.usd` translate; the only remaining warnings are the M4.3 pattern nodes (ramps, hex tiling, Voronoi) and quick_materials' real displacement (M4.6).

Notes on M4.1 as built:
- **Settings:** `RenderSettings::limits` (`core/scene/ray_limits.h`). The core default is no limit, so `maxDepth` alone caps, as in M2/M3, and every core test keeps its meaning. The delegate's defaults are Karma's (D13), with `nova:maxDepth` 64 as a safety cap. The render settings `nova:object:{diffuse,reflect,refract,sss,volume}Limit` and `nova:object:thinWallAtRefractLimit` are the defaults; the same names as constant primvars override them per mesh (`Scene::setMeshRayLimits`, no BLAS rebuild).
- **Integrator:**
  - Per path: four event counters packed in a `uint32`, a dither from the new path stream `kLimit`, and a `lightOnly` flag.
  - The event class (`ClosureSample::rayClass`) picks the counter. Within the limit the ray is full. Past it, the ray is light-only: pass A gathers crossed area lights and the environment, then drops it.
  - A refraction event between L and 2L continues straight (−wo) as a delta event without a medium change (thin wall).
  - The `maxDepth` iteration is light-only too, so its hits are no longer shaded. In M3 they were shaded only to add their emission (which Karma does not count): `cornell_rect` 3.95 s instead of 5.57 s.
- **Tests:** A1–A7 analytically, all deterministic: the furnace box Σρ^k for diffuse limits 0–3 (exact to 1e-4) plus fractional limits, the mirror corridor, glass stacks with and without thin walls, emission vs dome past the limit, per-object overrides both ways, sheen as diffuse and coat as reflection.
- **References:**
  - The scenes set equal limits for both renderers (`nova:object:*Limit` = `karma:object:*limit`, no `nova:maxDepth`).
  - Karma renders true caustics in all of them (`causticsenable 1`, clamp 0, in `_class_RefSettings`).
  - All Karma references were regenerated (self-checks passed). `furnace_materials` sets reflect and refract limits of 63, because its glass needs more than the default 4.

Notes on M4.2 as built:
- **Object property:** `nova:object:fakeCaustics` (render default and primvar, `RayLimitOverrides::fakeCaustics`). The scene resolves it per mesh at commit (`Scene::setDefaultFakeCaustics` from the render pass). Fake meshes are marked filtered; the opacity filter rejects them for occlusion queries.
- **Shadow rays:** phase 1 is the usual `occluded()`. Phase 2 runs only for unoccluded rays in scenes with fake meshes: closest hits along the segment (at most 8), each crossing's closure evaluated with RayType shadow, and the product of `closureTransmittance` (`core/bsdf/closure.h`).
- **Transmittance:**
  - Smooth `1 − F` at the outside angle with the index above 1 for both faces: no bending, and Fresnel reciprocity makes the exit equal the entry, so there is no false total reflection.
  - The R+T lobe's 1/E_RT compensation is undone, so roughness is ignored, as in Karma.
  - Tint and weight multiply. Thin walls keep their sheet transmission.
  - Deviation from Karma (D14): Karma darkens front faces by an extra (1 − F).
- **Paths after a diffuse event** (`afterDiffuse`):
  - At a fake mesh the glossy lobes are dropped and the transmission becomes a null event: straight on, weight T over its pick probability, MIS state kept.
  - Light-only rays cross fake meshes the same way, so the light and BSDF estimators agree.
- **Tests:**
  - shadow ratio 1 − F(45°) exactly, per-channel tint, two crossings T², thin wall (1−r)²/(1−r²), rough within 5e-3, per-object both ways, glass blocking with the setting off;
  - light-only vs BSDF-only through tinted glass under a dome within 2 % per channel.

Notes on M4.3 as built:
- **Ramps** (`kma_ramp_*`, `kma_rampconst_*`):
  - Lowered in IR: keys and values are parameters (value edits never recompile), the key count and the bases are structure. t clamps to the end values outside the keys.
  - Measured on Karma (`tests/karma/probe_ramps.py` → `tests/data/karma_ramps.json`); every basis below matches to 2e-5 unless noted:
    - constant and linear;
    - catmull-rom: uniform in the segment parameter, end values duplicated;
    - bspline: the uniform cubic B-spline of the points (key, value), end points tripled, solved for x(u) = t by 6 Newton steps;
    - bezier with 3k+1 keys: cubic segments of (key, value), solved the same way;
    - monotonecubic: Hermite with averaged index-space slopes, 0 at the ends. Houdini uses Kupan's slopes (`UT_Spline::getMonotoneSlopePA`, not public), so this is within 7e-3.
  - Other bases (hermite, incomplete Bezier) fall back to linear with one warning.
- **MikkT** (`karma_mikkt` on normal maps, UsdPreviewSurface, and graphs such as `hmtlxpbrtextureset`, which pass it to their inner normal maps):
  - N' = normalize(x T + y s (N×T) + z N), where T is the `tangentu` primvar (object space → world, unnormalized) and s is `signs` (−1 if missing). Without `tangentu`, Karma's uv tangent.
  - The Karma-measured cases match exactly. Nova reads the primvars directly; Karma only reads them when a geompropvalue also does.
- **Flakes:** a native `Flake` op (the 27-cell search with MaterialX's 4D cell noise, new in `noise.h`); the GGX flake normal is built in IR. Against Karma (`tests/karma/probe_flakes.py`, 32×32): presence, rand and id are bit-exact, the flake normal is within 2e-5.
- **Hex tiling:**
  - MaterialX `hextiledimage`/`hextilednormalmap` follow Karma's code path: the sign-keeping fmod hash, a −angle frame rotation, the gradient blend, and the lookups filtered with the unrotated derivatives × average scale (`IrBuilder::tex2D` with explicit derivatives). Nova vs Karma per pixel: ≤ 2e-4 (texture filtering).
  - Karma's own `kma_color_hextiling`/`kma_normalmap_hextiling` are native in Karma; their hash and parameter mapping are not public, and even with zero random rotation Karma offsets every tile randomly.
  - Nova maps them onto the same core: size → tiling, rot/rand_rot in turns, scale up to (1 + rand_scale), MaterialX offsets, contrast → falloff, contrast_falloff → luminance contrast, weightexp, seed → hash.
  - **Deviation:** the look is the same, the random tile layout differs.
- **Karma's graph library:**
  - `<houdini>/karmaNodeGraphs.json` becomes MaterialX nodegraphs of the kma nodedefs, with the graphs' default geometry properties (Pobject, Nobject, Tworld …).
  - `kma_hextiled_triplanar_*`, `kma_tangentrotate`, `kma_melanin` and `kma_deriv_2d_*` therefore expand like any nodegraph.
  - New natives: `kma_stderiv_*` (footprint differences solved against st's) and `kma_ocio_transform` (matrix transforms between spaces; others pass through with a warning).
- **Voronoi** (`kma_voronoinoise2d/3d`):
  - A native `Voronoi` op: F1 ≤ F2 ≤ F3 and the seed points in four metrics, position·freq − offset.
  - **Deviation:** the seed points are MaterialX's jittered cell points, not VEX vnoise's (not public), so the pattern differs from Karma's.
- **Coverage:**
  - Every material of Houdini's `basic_materials.usd` and `quick_materials.usd` translates without an unsupported node.

Notes on M4.4 as built:
- **Op:** `Op::Query` (`core/shading/query.h`): a = direction (3 wide), b = parameters (4 wide), imm = `QueryKind` (Occlusion 1 wide, Distance 4, Curvature 2, RoundedEdge 4).
  - Always varying. Never folded, even with constant operands. Excluded from derivative clones (a shifted clone reuses the base value).
  - `Program::needsQueries` marks programs that trace.
  - The VM hands each chunk (1, 8, 16 or 32 lanes) to `IShadingQueries::run` through `TextureContext`. Without an implementation it returns the defaults: displacement and opacity programs, the first-hit AOV pass, the IR evaluator and the GPU header check. The defaults are visibility 1, distance 1, curvature 0, and the direction for rounded normals.
- **Integrator:** `SceneShadingQueries` (`core/integrator/shading_queries.{h,cpp}`) traces all lanes' rays as one batch per call, as shadow rays for the hit filter.
  - Each hit's 3D sample comes from the new stream block `stream::shadingQuery(depth)` (ids from 0x10000, outside the full per-bounce slots). It shifts an R3 pattern, so one sample per hit is stratified across the pixel's samples, as Karma's is.
  - Programs without queries take none of this: no samples and no lanes. All 278 tests, the references included, are unchanged.
- **Semantics** (karma-nodes.md 4.3–4.6):
  - **AO:** cosine-weighted in the cone (sin²θ uniform up to sin² of the half angle) around nN, face-forwarded with `frontface`. The visibility is the unoccluded fraction within `maxdistance`.
    - Rays start on the side of the surface they leave, so with a tilted normal the rays below the surface see past it. This reproduces Karma's 0.4603 for the 45° normal; the analytic value is 0.4608.
  - **Distance:** uniform in the cone.
    - Mode 0 is the minimum, with misses at maxdistance.
    - Mode 1 is the mean over the rays that hit (no hits: maxdistance), divided by maxdistance with `normalize`.
    - `dir` is the nearest hit's direction (mode 0) or the mean hit direction (mode 1). It is zero without hits; Karma's value was not measured.
  - **Curvature:** `samples` uniform rays into the surface (convex) and `samples` out of it (concave), about the surface's own normal. Each gives the mean of max(0, 1 − L/maxdistance).
    - The help documents scale as "multiplies" and bias as "contrast control" with 0.5 neutral. Nova clamps scale·value to [0, 1] and applies Perlin's gain(bias); Karma's order of these steps was not measured.
    - space 0 = 0.5 + 0.5(convex − concave), space 1 = convex − concave.
  - **Rounded edge:** every sample traces three rays, along the normal and the two tangents, each through a disk of the radius perpendicular to its axis. The hits use multi-sample MIS with density |Ng·axis|. They are weighted by 1 − (d/r)²; this falloff fits Karma's measured profile to about 1° (`tests/karma/fit_roundededge.py`: 0.1/6.8/19.5/33.4° vs Karma 0.3/7.9/19.9/33.8°).
    - Hits within the cusp angle of the normal, and edges of the kind the mode excludes, count with the normal itself. The help defines the cusp angle as "below this threshold … won't be blended".
    - Convex vs concave: each surface lies below the other's tangent plane, tested as (N_hit − N)·(hit − P) > 0. This stays robust on the edge line.
    - The mask is 1 where a sample blended another surface. `scope:self` keeps only hits on the same mesh instance.
- **Translator:**
  - `kma_ao`, `kma_distance`, `kma_curvature` and `kma_roundededge` lower to the op. An unconnected or constant nN becomes the shading normal (Karma ignores authored constants); a connected nN is traced.
  - MaterialX's withdrawn `ambientocclusion` becomes the same AO, with 4 samples and the front face.
  - Coverage: 35 of 55 kma ids lower, and 740 of 746 MaterialX definitions.
- **Deviations:**
  - `traceset` and non-self `scope` trace everything, with a warning.
  - `kma_distance` `objectspace` 1 gives world distances, with a warning.
  - `frontface` 0 just leaves nN as is; Karma's measured values there fit no model (karma-nodes.md 4.3).
  - Nova's curvature is the exact model. Karma reads lower for small maxdistance/R (0.104 vs 0.125 at x = 0.5).
  - The rounded-edge mask finds the neighbouring face with probability (not certainty) per hit. At t = 0.022, r = 0.1 and 16 samples, more than 99 % of hits set it.
- **Tests:**
  - `test_shading_queries.cpp`:

Notes on M4.5 as built:
- **Structure** (`core/bsdf/closure_graph.h`): a POD `ClosureGraph` owned by the shading program (`Program::closure`, part of the structure hash; no padding, so the hash reads its bytes). It holds:
  - ≤ 48 nodes in post-order (leaf, mix, multiply, add, layer, layer over a VDF);
  - ≤ 8 emission terms, ≤ 4 frames and ≤ 4 segments.
  - Every number lives in the material's input block. `SurfaceInputs` grew to 384 floats: the native layout first, then leaf records, factors, segment layouts. Copies take only the floats in use (`SurfaceInputs::size`).
  - `finalizeClosureGraph` derives the structural flags from 64-bit descendant masks: leaves in a layer's top, reflections over refraction, refractive. The translator and the tests share it.
- **Builder** (`core/bsdf/graph_closure.h`, shares the native lobe helpers):
  - Per hit it makes two post-order passes, without recursion and DAG-safe: A bottom-up, then W top-down (bsdf-graphs.md 5.2, spec-linear per D5).
  - Interface leaves cache their albedo and compensation from the first pass for the push.
  - Leaves map onto the lobes:
    - oren_nayar becomes Lambert, QON or EON. **New `Burley` lobe** (MaterialX's `mx_burley_diffuse`, cosine sampled, Hill's albedo fit for picks; D6).
    - Translucent; subsurface (Lambert until M9).
    - Dielectric R / T / RT with film and thin walls. **T alone is normalized by 1/E_T (D7)**: `layer(R, T)` equals the native R+T lobe to 1e-4.
    - Conductor with film.
    - Generalized Schlick through the **new `FresnelModel::Hoffman`** (F0, F82, F90, exponent; `ConductorLobe::c2`). Its T and RT refract with f0_to_ior(average color0), warned.
    - Sheen, Charlie (capped like native) or Zeltner.
  - Seen from inside a refracting graph, T/RT leaves are the interface with 1/ior, and reflections over refraction drop out (D11).
  - Emission is the sum of the terms, each optionally scaled by generalized_schlick_edf's factor against the forward-facing normal. It is not attenuated by the layers.
  - LayerVdf sets `sigmaA` from the VDF's absorption.
- **Segments:**
  - `mix_surfaceshader` (and 1.38 `multiply_surfaceshader`; colours by their average, warned) make weighted segments, native or graph.
  - A single unweighted graph builds in place. Otherwise each segment builds into its own list, which is appended with its weight: frames after the previous ones, picks rescaled.
  - Emission, albedo and opacity mix linearly (opacity in IR). `sigmaA` comes from the segment that transmits most.
- **ClosureList:**
  - `kMaxLobes` 16, 4 frames, `nonDeltaMask` 16 bits.
  - On overflow it replaces the lobe with the smallest estimate when the new one is larger, and sets `overflow`. Its AOV share stays.
  - The translator warns once per material when a surface may exceed 16 lobes (leaves + 8 per native segment).

Notes on M4.6 as built:
- **Translation:**
  - The displacement terminal lowers to the output `surface_field::kDisplacement`, an object-space offset (3 floats).
  - `compile()` splits it off into `Program::displacement`, the graph reduced to that output, sharing the parameters and texture slots. The surface program never computes it.
  - Supported terminals:
    - `ND_displacement_float`: d·scale·N̂ₒ.
    - `ND_displacement_vector3`: scale·(x T̂ₒ + y B̂ₒ + z N̂ₒ), with Karma's frame built in object space from DPdu/DPdv.
    - `mix` displacement, `dot`, `surfacematerial.displacementshader`, and UsdPreviewSurface's `displacement` (d·N̂ₒ).
  - An unconnected zero amount or scale (Karma's default builder) emits nothing.
  - `Program::paramReads` lists the parameter floats a program reads (reachable Param nodes). Only those decide whether a value edit concerns the displacement.
- **Core:**
  - `tessellateSubdiv` takes per-edge rates (`TessellateOptions::edgeRate` per control edge, so it stays watertight; Bfr infers the inner rates) and reports split copies.
  - `core/geometry/dicing.h`: `edgePixels` (spherical distance to the eye; orthographic pixels per unit), `dicingRate` = clamp(ceil(px·q), 1, 1024), `boxInView`.
  - `core/geometry/displace.{h,cpp}` runs the displacement program once per point, in chunks of 32 lanes, with these rules:
    - The point's owner triangle (the first that references it) gives the material, the PrimId, DPdu/DPdv and the primvar corner. Instance primvars are unavailable (one BLAS per prototype).
    - The footprint is half the micropolygon's sides, the owner triangle's two shorter edges. That gives MIP level log2(texels/edge) − 1 (Karma −1.08). Shifted primvars move along the same edges.
    - Copies take their source's offset. The step-0 offset is added to every motion step (Karma/Cycles parity).
    - Normals are recomputed per step, area-weighted.
  - `displacementIsZero` detects uniform zero offsets.
- **Hydra:**
  - The material prim publishes a `HdNovaDisplacement` (program, uniforms for its parameter values, samplers) to the registry. Its hash covers the program structure, `paramReads` and the textures; it is null when the material does not displace.
  - The registry keeps material → mesh bindings. A changed displacement queues the bound meshes, and the render pass re-syncs them by path (`HdNovaResyncSceneIndex::DirtyPaths`) before rendering.
  - The render pass sets the **dicing view** at the first frame (eye, pixels per unit = |P₁₁|·height/2, world-to-clip). In the viewport camera moves do not re-dice (Karma's `ipr_continuous_dicing` off). A final render (husk, nova_cli: `houdini:viewport` unset) dices every frame from that frame's own camera, so a frame of a sequence is the frame rendered alone: a view other than the one in use re-syncs the displaced meshes, and the view is part of a view-diced mesh's signature. A mesh that is dirty in a new frame waits for the pass to confirm the view, with its dirty bits, and so dices once per frame (since 0.1.4; `docs/bug-audit-2026-11.md`, section 14).
  - Displaced meshes synced before that wait undisplaced and are re-synced once. This covers husk too: the camera is unknown at the first sync.
  - `HdNovaMesh`, with a displacing slot (`primvars:nova:displacement` ≠ false):
    - dices the control mesh: its scheme for subdivision meshes (which replaces the uniform level), bilinear on the authored faces for polygon meshes;
    - edge rates: ceil(px · `primvars:nova:dicingQuality` (0.5) · `nova:dicingQualityScale`), × `nova:offscreenQuality` (0.1) when the mesh's box is outside the frustum; or 2^`primvars:nova:dicingDepth`;
    - within `nova:subdivMaxTriangles`, fixed rates included (one warning);
    - displacement directions: limit normals for subdivision meshes; for polygon meshes the control normals (authored vertex/varying, faceVarying averaged, or angle-weighted face normals) interpolated bilinearly;
    - a signature of the slots' displacement hashes and the dicing settings decides rebuilds; a surface-only edit leaves the mesh alone.
  - `HdNovaSphere` with a displacing material becomes a unit cube cage diced at a quarter circle's pixel rate. Its points are projected onto the sphere (radius per motion step) and get the analytic sphere's st per corner, with seams unwrapped.
  - Settings: render settings `nova:dicingQualityScale` (1) and `nova:offscreenQuality` (0.1); per-prim primvars `nova:dicingQuality` (0.5), `nova:dicingDepth` and `nova:displacement`.
- **Measured** (`nova_cli`, orthographic, 300-px sphere): a polygon sphere with d = 0.1 gives silhouette radius 263.81 px vs 239.81 px undisplaced (1.1001). A UsdGeomSphere gives 264.00 vs 240.00 (1.1000).
